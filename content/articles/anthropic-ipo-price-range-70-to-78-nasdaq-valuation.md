Artificial intelligence developer Anthropic formally launched its initial public offering roadshow on Tuesday morning, filing an amended registration statement with the Securities and Exchange Commission to offer 825 million Class A shares at between 70 dollars and 78 dollars per share ahead of a planned November 19 debut on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker symbol ANT. According to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/anthropic-sets-price-range-of-70-to-78-in-upcoming-ipo.html) and [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/anthropic-seeks-14-trillion-valuation-in-ipo-130300928.html), pricing at the top of that band would raise 64.35 billion dollars in public proceeds while valuing the five year old creator of the Claude AI model family at roughly 1.02 trillion dollars on outstanding shares and up to 1.41 trillion dollars on a fully diluted basis.

Alongside the public share sale, Anthropic locked in an extraordinary 26 billion dollar concurrent private placement of non voting Class N stock from seven of the world's largest semiconductor and cloud computing companies at the exact IPO price: Nvidia, Microsoft, Alphabet's Google, Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company, Broadcom, Advanced Micro Devices, and Micron Technology. Combined with the public offering and a 30 day underwriter option for up to 123.75 million additional shares, total capital raised could surpass 90.4 billion dollars, rivaling or eclipsing Elon Musk's historic SpaceX public listing in June as the largest capital raising event in corporate history.

Even at those towering dimensions, Tuesday's filing reflects a deliberate valuation reset tailored to win over institutional buyers rattled by recent AI stock volatility and rising Treasury yields, coming down noticeably from the 2 trillion dollar figures Wall Street bankers floated in early autumn while still topping Anthropic's 965 billion dollar private funding mark from May.

## Breaking down the 825 million share offering and 20 percent retail target

Details inside Tuesday's amended S1 prospectus illustrate both the immense scale of the transaction and an aggressive push to bring individual investors into the syndicate. Of the 825 million Class A shares marketed on the roadshow, roughly 801.35 million are being issued directly by Anthropic to fund frontier compute clusters and enterprise expansion, while existing stockholders are selling approximately 23.65 million shares. At the 74 dollar midpoint of the range, Anthropic expects combined net proceeds from the public offering and the 26 billion dollar cornerstone private placement to reach roughly 84.6 billion dollars.

People familiar with the underwriting schedule told CNBC that bankers plan to price the deal on Wednesday, November 18, ahead of first Nasdaq trades on Thursday, November 19, noting that institutional order books are already covered across the 70 to 78 dollar range on an allocable basis with early institutional demand outpacing even SpaceX at the equivalent stage. Notably, Anthropic is targeting a retail investor allocation near 20 percent of the public float, double to quadruple the customary 5 percent to 10 percent retail slice in traditional technology listings.

Clarifying Tuesday's terms also resolved overnight confusion on Wall Street after an earlier draft term sheet briefly circulated among investment banks late Monday showing 867 million shares at up to 80 dollars per share before being retracted. Certain Anthropic board members and affiliated investment funds, backed historically by venture heavyweights including Sequoia Capital, Altimeter Capital, Dragoneer, and Greenoaks, also indicated interest in purchasing up to 303 million dollars of shares in the offering.

## Why seven hardware and cloud titans committed 26 billion dollars on the side

Securing 26 billion dollars in non voting Class N commitments from Google, Microsoft, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, and TSMC underscores how deeply circular and interdependent the artificial intelligence supply chain has become. Virtually every dollar Anthropic raises is destined to flow back across data center leases, custom silicon accelerators, high bandwidth memory stacks, wafer fabrication capacity, and [dedicated electrical generation commitments needed to power gigawatt scale AI campuses](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/anthropic-generator-commitments-ai-power/).

For chipmakers whose [data center power and accelerator revenue forecasts continue climbing sharply toward 2027](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/infineon-ai-data-center-power-chip-forecast-2027/), anchoring Anthropic's balance sheet guarantees that OpenAI's fiercest enterprise rival possesses sovereign scale liquidity to honor multi year hardware orders regardless of bond market turbulence. Accepting non voting Class N shares allows those seven strategic suppliers to participate financially at the IPO price without triggering antitrust governance vetoes or diluting public governance controls.

## Defining test for pure play generative AI valuations on Wall Street

Anthropic marches toward the Nasdaq bell at a uniquely revealing moment for technology investors. Unlike diversified hyperscalers Alphabet, Microsoft, and Amazon, which cushion massive capital expenditures with hundreds of billions of dollars in legacy search, software, and retail cash flows, or SpaceX, which blends orbital launch dominance and satellite broadband with AI ambitions, Anthropic offers public markets their first trillion dollar pure play test of frontier large language model economics.

Trimming initial 2 trillion dollar whispers down to roughly 1.02 trillion dollars on 13.09 billion outstanding shares and 1.26 trillion to 1.41 trillion dollars across 18.01 billion fully diluted shares shows Wall Street scrutinizing compute burn rates far more rigorously than six months ago. Yet if Anthropic successfully absorbs more than 90 billion dollars of public and strategic capital next week while rival OpenAI remains private, November 19 will stand as a watershed milestone in modern financial history.

## Revenue multiples and how Anthropic compares against SpaceX and OpenAI

Financial disclosures inside the amended prospectus also give public equity analysts concrete revenue benchmarks to weigh against Anthropic's trillion dollar price tag. Based on October revenue run rates cited by analysts reviewing the filing, pricing at 78 dollars per share values Anthropic at roughly thirteen times annualized revenue across its 13.09 billion outstanding shares and approximately seventeen times revenue on its 18.01 billion fully diluted share count.

By comparison, SpaceX raised roughly 75 billion to 86 billion dollars including overallotment options during its June debut at a valuation near 1.75 trillion dollars, backed by dominant commercial launch margins and tens of millions of global Starlink subscribers, while rival OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman reiterated earlier this autumn that OpenAI has no immediate timeline to go public until it can make firmer long term safety guarantees around frontier autonomous models. Stepping onto the Nasdaq stage ahead of OpenAI gives Anthropic first mover access to institutional index portfolios, sovereign wealth capital, and liquid acquisition currency, making next week's bookbuilding window between 70 and 78 dollars a defining verdict on the entire generative AI economy.
