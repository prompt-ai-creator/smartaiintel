Anthropic's stock is officially trading, and it jumped right out of the gate. Shares of the Claude maker opened on the Nasdaq at **$111.50** under the ticker **ANT** shortly before noon Eastern on Thursday, about **34% above** the $83 IPO price.

The first trade pushed Anthropic's value past **$2 trillion**, according to The New York Times, up from about $1.5 trillion at the IPO price and more than double what the company was worth six months ago.

## Update: Anthropic closes first day at $97.17

Anthropic finished its first day of trading at **$97.17**, up **17.1%** from the $83 IPO price, according to Nasdaq data reported by Yahoo Finance and Forbes.

The stock hit a high of **$115.10** shortly after its first trade, then slid through the afternoon to a low of **$92.72** before recovering into the close. The closing price is about 13% below the $111.50 opening trade. Roughly 840 million shares changed hands.

At the close, Forbes put Anthropic's market value at about **$1.27 trillion**, based on shares outstanding. On a fully diluted basis, which counts stock options and other securities, the company is worth closer to $1.76 trillion.

## Anthropic stock: the key numbers

- **Ticker:** ANT (Nasdaq Global Select Market)
- **IPO price:** $83 a share, set Wednesday night, above the $70 to $78 range
- **First trade:** $111.50, shortly before noon ET Thursday
- **First-day close:** $97.17, up 17.1%
- **Shares sold:** 980 million, up from the 825 million originally planned
- **Money raised:** about $81.3 billion, the largest IPO on record, or about $107.3 billion including a $26 billion private placement
- **Valuation:** about $1.5 trillion at $83, and more than $2 trillion at the first trade

The deal beat the $75 billion that SpaceX raised in June to become the biggest IPO ever by money raised, though SpaceX debuted at a higher valuation of about $1.77 trillion.

## Why trading started hours after the opening bell

CEO Dario Amodei and his sister, president Daniela Amodei, rang the Nasdaq opening bell at 9:30 a.m. ET. But the stock did not trade until much later.

That is normal for a big IPO. Nasdaq first runs an opening auction that collects buy and sell orders and finds a price that balances them. Ahead of the first trade, the indicated price climbed steadily, from about $105 in the morning to around $110 just before trading began.

## How to buy Anthropic stock

Now that ANT is listed, anyone with a regular brokerage account can buy it.

1. **Open or log in to a brokerage account.** Robinhood, Fidelity, Charles Schwab, E*Trade, Vanguard and most other major U.S. brokers list Nasdaq stocks.
2. **Search for the ticker "ANT."** Make sure it shows Anthropic, PBC.
3. **Choose how much to buy.** Many brokers let you buy fractional shares, so you do not need $111 or more to get started.
4. **Consider a limit order.** New listings can swing by large amounts in minutes. A limit order sets the highest price you are willing to pay, so you do not get filled at a sudden spike.

Financial advisers interviewed ahead of the debut said there is no rush to buy in the first minutes. Prices often settle after the first few days of trading. Options on ANT are expected to start trading as soon as Monday, Nov. 23.

**Note:** The shares that trade as ANT are Class A stock. Anthropic also sold separate non-voting Class N shares in its $26 billion private placement, which do not trade on the Nasdaq.

## What investors are buying

Anthropic, founded in 2021 by former OpenAI researchers, makes the Claude AI models and the Claude Code programming tool. Its revenue has exploded, from $386 million in 2024 to $4.6 billion in 2025 and $16.3 billion in just the first half of 2026, according to its prospectus.

It is also spending enormous sums. The company reported a large loss for the first half of 2026 and disclosed more than $450 billion in computing commitments through 2030.

Anthropic is the first company at the front of the generative AI boom to go public. Rival OpenAI has delayed its own IPO. That makes today's trading a closely watched test of investor appetite for AI, at a time when the Nasdaq has fallen about 4% this month on worries about the cost of the AI buildout.
