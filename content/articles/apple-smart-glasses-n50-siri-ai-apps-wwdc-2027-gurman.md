Apple is building its first smart glasses around a new version of Siri that can operate iPhone apps for you, and it hopes to show them off in June 2027, Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reported on Sunday.

"The idea: Book an Uber, reserve a table or order a coffee from your Apple Glasses — with apps running on paired iPhone," Gurman wrote in his weekly Power On newsletter.

The glasses, code-named N50, are what Apple hopes will be its flagship new product of 2027, according to the report. Apple has not announced or confirmed the device.

## Siri as the selling point

Apple sees its App Store as its biggest advantage over rival glasses from Meta and Google.

Instead of asking developers to build separate apps for the glasses, Apple wants them to add Siri controls, known as App Intents, to their existing iPhone apps. The apps would keep running on the iPhone, and the glasses would send them requests.

Examples cited in the reports include:

- Booking rides with **Uber** or **Lyft**
- Reserving tables through **OpenTable**
- Ordering at **Starbucks**
- Buying movie tickets on **Fandango**
- Shopping on **Amazon**

Apple has already started laying the groundwork. The first beta of iOS 27.3, released this month, introduces more detailed Siri controls for individual app functions. Apple's own apps will get the feature in the December release, with support for outside developers following in 2027.

## What the glasses will do

Beyond apps, Apple wants Siri to use the glasses' cameras to answer questions about what is around the wearer: identifying buildings, suggesting meals from what is in the fridge, giving directions and translating signs and conversations.

The glasses are also expected to handle calls, messages, iPhone notifications, calendar entries, reminders, music and smart home controls.

## No display, iPhone only

The first model will **not** have an augmented reality display, Gurman reported. Apple employees reportedly call the device "AirPods for your face".

Prototypes run a pared-down version of visionOS 28, have Wi-Fi, flush camera openings and a magnetic charger near the tip of the left arm. They are said to be slightly thinner than the second-generation Ray-Ban Meta glasses.

The glasses will need a recent iPhone and will not work with Android phones. Wi-Fi would let some features keep working when the iPhone is not close by, but the glasses will not be a fully independent device.

## The timeline

Apple aims to unveil the glasses at its Worldwide Developers Conference in June 2027 and start shipping them before the end of that year, according to the reports. Gurman cautioned that software delays make both dates uncertain.

Apple is also working on AirPods with cameras, which would give Siri a similar view of the user's surroundings. Those remain on track for late 2027.

The plan puts Apple in direct competition with Meta, which already sells Ray-Ban smart glasses with third-party app integrations, and with Google, which has shown glasses that can book Uber rides and prepare DoorDash orders using its Gemini assistant.
