WASHINGTON — Federal auto safety regulators issued an urgent consumer alert on Friday urging the owners of 720,796 BMW sedans, convertibles, and sport utility vehicles across the United States to park outdoors and away from homes or garages immediately because an engine component can short-circuit and ignite even when the car is turned off.

According to recall filings published by the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration under campaign number 26V733000, an improperly sealed electrical connector on the engine water pump can degrade over time and allow moisture to seep inside, triggering an electrical short and under-hood fire hazard while a vehicle sits unattended.

## Why vehicles already fixed in 2024 must be repaired a second time

What makes Friday's directive unusually alarming for owners is that every affected vehicle was previously recalled in 2024 for the exact same water-pump hazard—and BMW does not yet have a permanent replacement fix ready to install.

Under the earlier 2024 campaign, BMW dealerships fitted a protective shield intended to divert residual fluid away from the water pump's electrical plug. Subsequent engineering investigations launched after customers reported fresh thermal events revealed that the shield failed to prevent moisture intrusion. BMW told federal regulators it is aware of approximately 10 field incidents in the United States—including smoldering and overheating—that occurred in vehicles *after* they had already received the 2024 recall remedy, though no crashes or injuries have been reported.

Because engineers are still designing a new hardware solution, NHTSA instructed drivers to park outside and away from residential structures and neighboring cars until dealerships can perform the updated repair free of charge.

## Which BMW cars and SUVs are covered—and what owners should do next

Spanning 53 distinct trim configurations from the 2012 through 2018 model years, the park-outside advisory covers a broad cross-section of BMW's core U.S. lineup:

- **Sedans, Coupes, and Convertibles:** Certain 2012–2016 BMW 3 Series sedans (including 2014–2016 328xi models), 2012–2016 5 Series sedans, 2012–2016 Z4 roadsters, 2015–2016 4 Series Gran Coupes, and 2015–2016 2 Series Convertibles.
- **Crossovers and SUVs:** Certain 2012–2015 BMW X1, 2013–2017 X3, 2015–2018 X4, and 2016–2018 X5 xDrive40e plug-in hybrid SUVs.

BMW estimates that fewer than 0.1 percent of recalled vehicles physically exhibit the defect, but both the automaker and NHTSA stressed that indoor garage fires pose catastrophic household risks—particularly at a moment when [stubbornly high U.S. mortgage rates and repair costs are already straining American homeowners](/articles/us-existing-home-sales-plunge-lowest-level-since-2010-mortgage-rates/) and [automakers navigate broader industry turbulence](/articles/tesla-cybertruck-deposit-refunds-production-end/).

Interim notification letters warning owners of the fire risk are scheduled to begin mailing on Dec. 25, 2026, the same date affected vehicle identification numbers will become searchable on NHTSA's recall portal and SaferCar mobile app, with a second letter to follow once replacement parts reach dealerships. Concerned drivers can contact BMW customer service at 1-800-525-7417 or NHTSA's Vehicle Safety Hotline at 1-888-327-4236.
