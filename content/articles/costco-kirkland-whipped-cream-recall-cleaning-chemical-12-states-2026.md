Costco shoppers in 12 states are being told to check their fridges before Thanksgiving: some cans of Kirkland Signature Real Heavy Whipped Cream may contain a small amount of a cleaning chemical.

The maker, Alamance Foods, is voluntarily recalling the cans "out of an abundance of caution", it said in a notice to Costco members. The recall was announced on November 7 and is drawing fresh attention as families shop for the holiday on Thursday, November 26.

"We have learned that one batch of Kirkland Signature Whipped Cream may contain a small amount of a cleaning chemical," the company said. "Members have reported a chemical smell and taste, but no serious adverse effects requiring medical care have been reported."

## How to check your cans

Look for **all** of the following on the can or packaging:

| Detail | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Product | Kirkland Signature Real Heavy Whipped Cream, 15 oz (425 g) |
| Costco item number | 1777000 |
| Manufacturer item code | AC50 |
| Best-if-used-by date | April 2, 2027 |
| Timestamp on the can | 00:35 to 02:45 |
| Purchase dates | October 11 to October 31, 2026 |

The cans were sold in three-packs, with eight three-packs to a case.

## Which states

The recalled cans were sold at Costco warehouses in:

- Alabama
- Florida
- Georgia
- Illinois
- Indiana
- Iowa
- Mississippi
- Missouri
- North Carolina
- South Carolina
- Tennessee
- Wisconsin

The recall covers only this batch. There has been no announcement of a nationwide recall of all Kirkland Signature whipped cream.

## What to do

Stop using any cans that match, even if you have not noticed an unusual smell or taste. Return them to any Costco warehouse for a full refund.

Costco is also contacting members who bought the product through its purchase records. Questions can be sent to Alamance Foods at customer.care@alamancefood.com.

## What we don't know

Alamance Foods has not said which cleaning chemical was involved, how it got into the batch or how many cans were affected.

Because the cans carry a best-by date in April 2027, many may still be sitting in refrigerators, which is why the recall matters so close to Thanksgiving, when whipped cream tops millions of pumpkin pies.
