Cyclone Akvan is strengthening over the Bay of Bengal and is on course to hit India's Andhra Pradesh coast this week as a "very severe" cyclonic storm, the India Meteorological Department (IMD) said, as neighbouring Odisha deployed dozens of rescue teams and began moving people out of danger.

The IMD forecasts landfall between Nellore and Tuni around noon on Thursday, November 20 (about 6:30 a.m. UTC), with sustained winds of 120-130 km/h (75-80 mph) gusting to 145 km/h (90 mph), roughly the strength of a Category 1 hurricane.

If it makes landfall as forecast, Akvan will be the third cyclone to hit Andhra Pradesh in about three weeks.

## Where the storm is now

In its latest bulletin, issued at 2:45 a.m. India time on Tuesday, the IMD said Akvan had stayed practically stationary for six hours. At 11:30 p.m. on Monday it lay over the central Bay of Bengal, about 610 km east of Chennai and 670 km south-southeast of Visakhapatnam, with winds of 65-75 km/h gusting to 85 km/h.

The deep depression was upgraded to a cyclonic storm and named Akvan on Monday evening. The IMD expects it to become a severe cyclonic storm on Tuesday evening, November 17, and a very severe cyclonic storm by Thursday morning, before turning northwest towards the Andhra Pradesh coast.

## Odisha deploys 74 rescue teams and begins evacuations

Odisha has deployed 60 Odisha Disaster Rapid Action Force (ODRAF) teams and 14 National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) teams ahead of the storm.

District collectors have been told to identify vulnerable locations, open relief centres and evacuate residents from landslide-prone areas in Ganjam, Gajapati, Kandhamal, Rayagada, Koraput and Malkangiri districts. Pregnant women are being moved to safety and reservoirs are being drawn down to make room for heavy rain, measures the state says reflect lessons from deadly landslides during the previous cyclone.

"The present cyclone is likely to make landfall between Nellore and Tuni in Andhra Pradesh on the afternoon of November 20," Odisha Revenue and Disaster Management Minister Suresh Pujari told reporters.

Ganjam, Gajapati and Puri are under an orange alert for heavy to very heavy rain on November 19. On landfall day, Malkangiri, Koraput, Rayagada, Gajapati and Ganjam are under an orange alert.

## Heavy rain for Chennai and Tamil Nadu

In Tamil Nadu, which has stayed largely dry this northeast monsoon season, the IMD forecast heavy rain with thunder and lightning in Chennai, Tiruvallur and Chengalpattu from Tuesday, with gusty winds of 40-50 km/h. Heavy to very heavy rain is possible at isolated places across north Tamil Nadu and Puducherry on November 18 and 19.

Coastal Andhra Pradesh faces heavy to very heavy rain on November 19 and 20, with isolated extremely heavy falls possible in the north coastal districts. After landfall, heavy rain is forecast to spread inland to eastern Telangana on November 20 and to Vidarbha and east Madhya Pradesh on November 21.

Sri Lankan forecasters say the storm will have no direct impact on the island.

## A third blow for Andhra Pradesh in a month

Akvan follows Cyclones Arnab and Murasu, which made landfall near Narasapur on October 28 and near Ongole on November 2. The two storms caused losses of around ₹6,500 crore (roughly $750 million) in Andhra Pradesh and flattened standing crops across more than 450,000 hectares.

Fishermen, many still waiting for compensation for Arnab's losses, have been told to stay ashore until November 20.

## Why it is called Akvan

Akvan is a name contributed by Iran to the list used for cyclones in the north Indian Ocean. In the Persian epic the Shahnameh, Akvan is a demon who hurls the hero Rustam into the sea.
