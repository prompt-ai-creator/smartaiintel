A magnitude 4.2 earthquake jolted the Los Angeles area before dawn on Friday, waking people across the region with a short, sharp jolt.

The quake struck at **3:25 a.m. PT** about a mile northwest of **Commerce**, roughly 5 miles east-southeast of downtown Los Angeles, according to the [U.S. Geological Survey](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/ci41364111). Its magnitude estimate moved between 4.0 and 4.4 in the first hours before the USGS settled on 4.2.

"That earthquake was strong! Haven't felt one like that in a while," one person wrote on social media, [BNO News reported](https://bnonews.com/index.php/2026/11/magnitude-4-4-earthquake-strikes-near-downtown-los-angeles/). Others described a brief, sharp jolt.

## Update: USGS lists the quake at 4.2, no damage reported

The U.S. Geological Survey now lists Friday's earthquake as a **magnitude 4.2**, after its review of the data. Early estimates ranged from 4.0 to 4.4, and the USGS briefly listed it as a 4.4 before settling back at 4.2. Magnitudes often shift like this in the first hours after a quake. Many outlets, including the [Los Angeles Times](https://www.latimes.com/california/story/2026-11-20/4-2-magnitude-earthquake-rattles-la-area-centered-near-commerce), reported it as a 4.4 earlier on Friday. The USGS also put the depth at about 9.6 miles.

City officials say there was no damage. "While unsettling, there are no immediate reports of damage or operational impacts, including at LAX," Los Angeles Mayor Karen Bass posted on X, [NewsNation reported](https://www.newsnationnow.com/weather/magnitude-4-earthquake-los-angeles-county/). The Los Angeles Fire Department, which sent crews from all 106 neighborhood fire stations to check critical infrastructure, stayed in earthquake mode until shortly before 4:30 a.m., [ABC7 reported](https://abc7.com/post/magnitude-42-earthquake-strikes-commerce-area-usgs-says/20058854/). There were no immediate reports of serious injuries.

More than **12,000 people** have now reported feeling the quake. Residents in Koreatown, Inglewood, Silver Lake, Mar Vista and Arcadia reported brief shaking, according to the Times, and ABC7 said it was also felt in Marina del Rey, Hawthorne, Van Nuys and Pico Rivera. No aftershocks had been recorded near the epicenter as of 5:30 a.m. PT.

The USGS has not named the fault involved. Its early data show thrust motion, in which one block of rock is pushed up over another, a common type of movement beneath the Los Angeles basin.

## Where the shaking was felt

Within half an hour, more than **7,500 people** had reported feeling the quake through the USGS [Did You Feel It? system](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/ci41364111/tellus). Reports came from across Los Angeles County, including East Los Angeles, South Pasadena, Inglewood and Long Beach, and stretched to Santa Clarita, Simi Valley and Huntington Beach.

The strongest shaking was near the epicenter, where the USGS ShakeMap shows light shaking, level IV on the intensity scale. At that level, people indoors feel it clearly, windows and dishes may rattle, and parked cars may rock, but damage is rare.

## Any damage?

The USGS issued a **green PAGER alert**, its lowest level, which means little or no damage is expected from a quake of this size. Police were heard on the radio telling all units to check their assigned areas for damage, [according to Metro](https://metro.co.uk/2026/11/20/buildings-shake-los-angeles-rocked-powerful-earthquake-29806758/).

The earthquake was centered inland, about 15 miles from the coast, and was far too small to cause a tsunami.

## A second California quake in hours

The Los Angeles quake came about five hours after a stronger [magnitude 5.0 earthquake](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/nc75468702) struck near **Pinnacles** in Central California at 10:49 p.m. PT Thursday, about 25 miles east of Salinas. Nearly 2,900 people reported feeling that one, and it was followed by a string of smaller quakes in the same area.

The two quakes were about 250 miles apart, and scientists have not linked them.

## What to do if there are aftershocks

Smaller aftershocks are common after an earthquake and can be felt for days. None had been recorded near Commerce in the first half hour.

If you feel shaking, seismologists advise you to **Drop, Cover and Hold On**: get down on your hands and knees, take cover under a sturdy table or desk, and hold on until the shaking stops. Stay away from windows, and do not run outside during the shaking.

California's earthquake early warning system sends phone alerts through the MyShake app and Android phones, but usually only when it estimates a quake at magnitude 4.5 or higher, [the Los Angeles Times has explained](https://www.latimes.com/california/story/2024-08-12/california-earthquake-early-warning-system-how-to-get-it-now). That means many people may not have received a warning for Friday's quake.

It has been a busy month for California's weather and natural hazards, after [huge waves battered the coast](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/huge-waves-hit-california-coast-mavericks-50-feet-pacifica-flooding-santa-cruz-2026/) earlier in November.
