WASHINGTON — The U.S. Justice Department and the FBI announced a sweeping three-part cybercrime crackdown on Thursday, securing court orders to seize more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, dismantling North Korea's $400 million "FaceStealer" webcam-hijacking network, and seizing U.S. domains powering the Gootloader ransomware botnet.

The coordinated actions represent the largest single-day virtual-asset recovery and malware infrastructure takedown unveiled by federal prosecutors in 2026, pairing cryptocurrency forfeiture teams in Washington and Los Angeles with international cyber units in Colorado and Europe.

## Freezing $1.2 billion from the historic Binance breach

Following the Oct. 29 theft of more than $6 billion in digital assets from Binance—attributed by the FBI to a North Korean military hacking unit tracked as TraderTraitor—U.S. prosecutors and private virtual-asset exchanges moved immediately to freeze more than $2.7 billion before launderers could route the funds through cross-chain bridges and mixers.

Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva and District of Columbia U.S. Attorney Jeanine Ferris Pirro said Thursday that the Justice Department has already obtained federal court authorization to seize or restrain in excess of $1.2 billion of that frozen total, working alongside the U.S. Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation to trace remaining wallets.

## Inside North Korea's 'FaceStealer' webcam and deepfake trap

In Los Angeles, federal prosecutors and FBI Cyber Division agents executed warrants seizing cloud servers and more than 900 secretly recorded victim webcam videos tied to "FaceStealer," a North Korean social-engineering campaign attributed to APT38, Sapphire Sleet, and BlueNoroff that stole over $400 million in cryptocurrency in the past 18 months.

According to an FBI technical advisory and a new federal portal at fbi.gov/facestealer, operatives compromise an initial Web3 engineer or executive, silently record their webcam feed, and hijack their messaging accounts to invite real-world colleagues onto an imposter video call. Targets see prerecorded video of their trusted contact—or AI-generated deepfakes of prominent crypto figures—before a fake audio-error prompt tricks them into installing wallet-draining malware.

FBI Los Angeles Assistant Director in Charge Patrick Grandy warned that North Korean hackers are increasingly aiming FaceStealer lures at open-source software maintainers to compromise code repositories used by millions of downstream users.

## Gootloader ransomware botnet servers seized in Operation Endgame

In a third coordinated enforcement action led out of the District of Colorado with Europol, Army CID, and the Defense Criminal Investigative Service, court-authorized operations removed Gootloader malware from more than 100 compromised servers and replaced criminal U.S. command domains with official Justice Department seizure banners.

Unsealed warrants show the Gootloader botnet infected at least 25,000 computers worldwide since 2018—including targets inside the U.S. defense industrial base—and enabled ransomware attacks responsible for roughly $28.8 million in losses across 44 U.S. victims. Combined with Thursday's [Operation TorNado dark-web raids seizing $234 million](/articles/fbi-europol-operation-tornado-354-arrests-234-million-seized/) and recent [enterprise zero-day patch advisories](/articles/microsoft-patches-1259-flaws-outlook-zero-day-railway-ai-key-breach/), federal officials signaled an aggressive escalation against state-backed and ransomware infrastructure.
