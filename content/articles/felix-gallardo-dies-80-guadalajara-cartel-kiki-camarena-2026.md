Miguel Ángel Félix Gallardo, the Mexican drug lord known as "El Jefe de Jefes", or the Boss of Bosses, has died at the age of 80.

He died on Sunday at a hospital in Guadalajara, in western Mexico, the Jalisco state security secretariat said. Officials said he died at 2 p.m. local time of "multiple organ failure".

Félix Gallardo had been taken to hospital on an emergency basis on Friday from Puente Grande prison, where he had been held since 1989.

## The "Boss of Bosses"

Born in Culiacán, Sinaloa, in 1946, Félix Gallardo co-founded the Guadalajara cartel with Rafael Caro Quintero and Ernesto Fonseca Carrillo.

In the 1980s it was the most powerful criminal organisation in Mexico, controlling smuggling routes along the US border. It was among the first Mexican groups to work with Colombian cocaine suppliers, according to the DEA. Several later groups, including the Sinaloa cartel, emerged from it.

## The murder of Kiki Camarena

Félix Gallardo is remembered in the United States above all for the death of Enrique "Kiki" Camarena, an agent of the US Drug Enforcement Administration.

Camarena was kidnapped in Guadalajara in February 1985, then tortured and killed. He was 37. The killing triggered Operation Leyenda, which the DEA describes as the largest homicide investigation it had ever undertaken, and badly strained relations between Washington and Mexico City.

Félix Gallardo was among the major crime figures arrested over Camarena's murder. He was detained in Mexico on April 8, 1989.

He was first sentenced to 40 years in prison. In 2017 a Mexican federal judge added a 37-year term for the murders of Camarena and Mexican pilot Alfredo Zavala Avelar, bringing his total to 77 years.

## Years of failing health

In prison, Félix Gallardo complained of poor conditions and serious health problems, including vertigo, deafness and the loss of an eye. He was moved from a maximum-security prison to a medium-security facility in Guadalajara in 2014 because of his health.

From 2019 he repeatedly asked to serve the rest of his sentence at home, citing his age. The courts turned him down. In September 2022 a judge finally granted house arrest, but the order was never carried out because Mexico's Attorney General's Office appealed, according to the Associated Press.

## A story told by Narcos

His life reached a global audience through the Netflix series *Narcos: Mexico*, in which he was played by Diego Luna. The show's first season centred on the rise of the Guadalajara cartel and the Camarena case.

His death comes as Washington steps up pressure on Mexico's cartels. His former partner Caro Quintero, who was released from prison in 2013 and recaptured in 2022, was sent to the United States in 2025 to face charges, including over Camarena's murder.
