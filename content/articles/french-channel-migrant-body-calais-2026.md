A man in his 20s has died following an attempted small boat crossing of the English Channel, the French coastguard has reported. His body was discovered on **Sainte-Cecile beach**, near the coastal town of Le Touquet in northern France, on Saturday morning — adding to a rising death toll among migrants attempting one of the world's most dangerous migrant crossings.

## The incident: What happened Saturday morning

French authorities said an overnight rescue team from the **Regional Operational Center for Surveillance (CROSS)** had been deployed to monitor migrants boarding a "makeshift vessel" at the beach. Authorities stopped an overloaded dinghy from departing.

A small craft, along with land-based units from the local fire and rescue service, conducted "a thorough sweep of the area but found no one in the water."

Following the operation, at approximately **08:00 local time (09:00 GMT)**, the body of a man in his twenties was discovered on the beach.

Five men were attended to by firefighters at the scene for signs of **hypothermia** after the boarding attempt was foiled.

## A rising death toll

The death adds to a mounting toll among migrants attempting the dangerous crossing between France and England.

In the latest week of data, ending 8 November, the UK government reported that **903 migrants arrived by small boat into the UK in 14 boats**.

More than **60 people have died** trying to cross the Channel to England in increasingly overcrowded dinghies since the start of 2026, making this year the deadliest since migrant crossings began in earnest.

The Channel crossing has become one of the most politically sensitive issues in both the UK and France. Small, overcrowded dinghies attempt the busy shipping lane in all weather conditions, often at night, with migrants paying smugglers for passage. The waters are cold even in summer, and hypothermia can set in quickly.

## French response and investigation

The French coastguard has said an investigation will be opened to "determine the circumstances surrounding the incident." CROSS teams continue to monitor northern French beaches overnight, and local fire and rescue services remain on standby to respond to overloaded or distressed vessels.

French authorities have faced increasing pressure to prevent departures, while UK authorities have pushed for stronger enforcement on the French side of the Channel. The two governments have negotiated multiple agreements over the years to reduce crossings, yet the number of attempts and deaths continues to rise.
