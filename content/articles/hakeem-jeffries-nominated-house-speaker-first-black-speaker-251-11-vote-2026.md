House Democrats nominated Hakeem Jeffries for speaker on Wednesday, putting the New York Democrat on track to become the first Black speaker of the House in US history.

Jeffries won 251 of the 262 ballots cast in a closed-door, secret-ballot vote, with 11 members voting against him. He ran unopposed. The full House will vote on the speakership when the new Congress convenes on Jan. 3, 2027.

With Democrats set to hold one of the largest House majorities in decades after this month's midterm elections, that vote is widely seen as a formality. The speaker is second in line to the presidency, after the vice president.

## A recorded vote, not acclamation

Some Jeffries allies had hoped to nominate him by acclamation, without a formal ballot. Jeffries instead asked for a recorded vote, arguing that every Democrat should have the chance to weigh his record, AFP reported. The ballot stayed secret, so only the overall tally was made public.

The 251-11 result shows that Jeffries has the overwhelming support of his caucus, despite pressure from some newly elected progressives who wanted new leadership after the midterms. Phone calls and social media posts calling for a fresh start had flooded the offices of Democrats, and protesters heckled Jeffries last week at a Democratic conference in Denver, the Associated Press reported.

Before the vote, Jeffries met with the caucuses that make up House Democrats and talked to almost all members. "I embrace those conversations," he said. He also met with progressive lawmakers on issues including Israel and healthcare, including proposals to lower the Medicare eligibility age.

"There is one candidate in this race: Hakeem Jeffries," Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez said at the Capitol on Tuesday.

Rep. Steven Horsford of Nevada, a close Jeffries ally, said the caucus was united. "What I know about Leader Jeffries is he's a consensus builder. He's a listener, and he's about building coalition," Horsford told reporters.

## A historic leadership team

Jeffries is part of a leadership trio with Rep. Katherine Clark of Massachusetts and Rep. Pete Aguilar of California, who also ran unopposed. Clark is in line to become the first woman to serve as House majority leader, and Aguilar the first Latino majority whip. Rep. Ted Lieu of California is in line to become Democratic caucus chair.

The three have risen together as the first generation of Democratic leaders after Nancy Pelosi, and will lead in the majority for the first time.

On the Republican side, [Jim Jordan beat Steve Scalise to become House minority leader](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/jim-jordan-elected-house-minority-leader-defeats-scalise/) for the new Congress.

## Who is Hakeem Jeffries?

Jeffries, 56, was raised in Brooklyn's working-class Crown Heights neighborhood and trained as a lawyer. He entered the New York state legislature in 2007 and won election to Congress in 2012.

In 2022, he succeeded Pelosi as the top House Democrat, becoming the first Black lawmaker to lead a party in either chamber of Congress. He also served as a House prosecutor during Trump's first impeachment trial in 2020.

## What comes next

As speaker, Jeffries would lead a Democratic majority during the final two years of President Donald Trump's term. Democrats have promised investigations into the Trump administration, and the White House has already [warned incoming Oversight Committee chair Robert Garcia](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/white-house-warns-oversight-chair-robert-garcia-trump-probes-criminality/) about the probes.

Jeffries will also have to hold together a caucus that ranges from centrists to an increasingly assertive progressive wing, while Trump keeps his veto power.

Democrats will also control the Senate in January, after [Mary Peltola's win in Alaska gave the party 56 seats](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/mary-peltola-wins-alaska-senate-beats-dan-sullivan-democrats-56-seats-2026/). Senate Democrats face a more competitive leadership contest, with Chuck Schumer challenged by Maryland Sen. Chris Van Hollen.
