Persistent Waikato rain washed out Tuesday's third One Day International between New Zealand and India at Seddon Park in Hamilton without a single delivery being bowled, preserving India's unbeatable 2 to 0 lead in the five match series while handing the Black Caps a narrow mathematical lifeline heading to Mount Maunganui. According to [India Today's match report](https://www.indiatoday.in/sports/cricket/story/india-vs-new-zealand-third-odi-washout-hamilton-rain-series-alive-3035869-2026-11-10), passing morning showers and a soaked outfield initially delayed the scheduled afternoon toss by more than two hours before a brief dry window raised hopes of a shortened forty over contest.

During that fleeting break in the clouds, Indian captain Shubman Gill won the coin toss for the third consecutive game on tour, elected to bowl first under heavy grey skies, and confirmed an unchanged Indian playing eleven featuring veteran batters Rohit Sharma and Virat Kohli. Yet just as Seddon Park ground staff began peeling back the main hessian and plastic covers to prepare the square for a reduced forty overs per side fixture, steady rain swept back across Hamilton and never relented.

Once the final cut off time for even a minimum twenty over contest passed with standing moisture across the outfield and steady drizzle still falling, match officials consulted with both dressing rooms and officially abandoned the match. Disappointed supporters filed out of Seddon Park without seeing Kohli, Rohit, or New Zealand's seamers take the field, marking the third straight weather disrupted fixture of a sodden November white ball tour.

## How Duckworth Lewis Stern calculations shaped India's 2 to 0 series stranglehold

Although Hamilton produced the first complete washout of the tour, rain clouds have hovered over every chapter of this five match bilateral contest and repeatedly tested both squads' tactical awareness of Duckworth Lewis Stern par tables. In the series opener in Auckland, persistent interruptions trimmed play to 32 overs per side, where India's bowlers restricted the hosts effectively before Indian batters chased down a revised target of 143 with five wickets in hand to draw first blood.

Even finer margins decided the second One Day International. New Zealand's towering pace attack fought back fiercely through seamers Jacob Duffy and Kyle Jamieson, who claimed three wickets apiece to reduce India to a precarious 113 for 7 during a tense chase. When heavy rain halted play for good at that exact juncture, however, scoreboard awareness proved decisive: India sat four runs ahead of the Duckworth Lewis Stern par score, clinching a dramatic four run victory that doubled the tourists' advantage to 2 to 0.

Tuesday's abandonment in Hamilton locks in two major series consequences at once. First, India can no longer lose the five match rubber, needing just a single victory from the final two outings to seal an outright series triumph in New Zealand. Second, the washout officially ends New Zealand's proud streak of consecutive home bilateral ODI series victories, as the Black Caps' best possible result from here is winning both closing fixtures to salvage a two apiece draw.

## Shreyas Iyer steps in as captain as Shubman Gill departs for Test preparations

When the two squads reconvene at Bay Oval in Mount Maunganui for the fourth ODI on Friday, November 13, and the fifth ODI on Sunday, November 15, both sides will field noticeably reshaped lineups. On a day when [international cricket headlines also centered on sharp governance debates across the Tasman in Australia](https://www.bbc.com/sport/cricket/articles/cjzxlxrp3335o), Indian team management confirmed planned workload rotations designed to prioritize red ball readiness ahead of the upcoming Test series.

Skipper Shubman Gill and wicketkeeper batter KL Rahul are scheduled to depart the white ball group to begin dedicated red ball preparation, handing the Indian captaincy to vice captain Shreyas Iyer for the two Bay Oval encounters. Crucially for spectators on New Zealand's North Island coast and television audiences worldwide, senior stalwarts Rohit Sharma and Virat Kohli remain available for both Mount Maunganui games, giving Iyer formidable top order experience alongside incoming fringe squad members eager to press white ball selection claims.

New Zealand's selectors are executing a parallel split squad transition of their own, releasing several multi format regulars toward Test match preparation while refreshing their white ball group for a must win coastal double header. Having watched Duffy and Jamieson expose Indian middle order vulnerability with swinging new balls in game two before weather intervened, the hosts still believe back to back victories at Bay Oval are well within reach if clear skies finally allow fifty over cricket.

## Coastal weather watch ahead of Friday's fourth ODI at Bay Oval

Attention now shifts northeast from landlocked Hamilton to Mount Maunganui, where Bay Oval's sand based drainage system traditionally recovers far faster from passing Pacific showers than older inland turf venues. Even so, after rain shortened the first two matches and wiped out the third entirely, captains Iyer and his New Zealand counterpart know toss decisions and powerplay run rates will remain paramount on Friday.

Fielding first has been Gill's automatic call across all three coin flips so far because chasing with exact Duckworth Lewis Stern par numbers illuminated on the scoreboard confers a massive strategic edge whenever late afternoon clouds gather. For India, one clean afternoon of batting rhythm from Rohit, Kohli, and Iyer at Bay Oval will wrap up a hard earned overseas trophy; for New Zealand, zero margin for error remains if they hope to send Sunday's finale to a decider.

## What the split squad audition means for both dressing rooms

Rotating Shubman Gill and KL Rahul out ahead of the upcoming red ball series turns the two Bay Oval fixtures into a high value white ball audition for India's depth batters and wicketkeeping understudies. Having Rohit Sharma and Virat Kohli anchored at the top of the order allows Shreyas Iyer to slot newcomers into clearly defined middle overs roles without sacrificing veteran calm against New Zealand's disciplined new ball seamers.

For Iyer personally, captaining an overseas bilateral clincher carries significant leadership weight inside Indian cricket's multi format hierarchy. Already praised by domestic observers for composed field placements and aggressive spin bowling changes in white ball tournaments, sealing a 3 to 0 or 3 to 1 series triumph in Mount Maunganui would reinforce his credentials as India's primary tactical deputy whenever Test workloads pull Gill away from fifty over assignments.

New Zealand face an equally revealing examination of domestic batting depth. Losing their streak of consecutive home bilateral ODI series wins after back to back Duckworth Lewis Stern defeats stung a proud white ball unit accustomed to exploiting seam movement and short straight boundaries on home soil. Even with senior Test regulars stepping aside, bowlers Jacob Duffy and Kyle Jamieson proved in game two that probing lengths can still rattle India's middle order when early wickets expose new batters to laterally moving deliveries. Turning those bursts into a complete fifty over victory at Bay Oval on Friday is now the sole path left to keep Sunday's series finale alive.
