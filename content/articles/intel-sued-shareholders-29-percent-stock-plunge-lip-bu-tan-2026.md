Intel has been sued by shareholders who say the chipmaker hid the damage from a slowdown in personal computer sales before its stock fell nearly 29% in a single day.

The proposed class action was filed on Friday in federal court in San Jose, California. It is led by the City of Southfield Fire and Police Retirement System, a Michigan pension fund, and names Chief Executive Lip-Bu Tan and Chief Financial Officer David Zinsner as defendants alongside the company, Reuters reported.

## The plunge

Intel shares fell 28.6% on October 30, wiping out about $140 billion in market value.

The day before, Intel had forecast fourth-quarter revenue and profit below Wall Street's expectations and warned of a prolonged slowdown in PC sales. Tan told analysts he was disappointed by manufacturing supply shortages, an "unprecedented memory crunch" and the resulting softness in PCs.

"We need to address these challenges with urgency," Tan said.

## What the shareholders claim

The lawsuit says Intel was too slow to tell investors how slowing PC sales and an inventory glut would weigh on revenue.

The plaintiffs argue those problems outweighed the benefits Intel was getting from data centre construction and the wider artificial intelligence boom.

They also say Intel had a motive to keep the bad news quiet. In mid-August, halfway through what the suit calls a "disastrous" third quarter, Intel sold $23 billion of stock at $95 a share. The sale was part of Tan's push to build up Intel's contract chipmaking business.

The proposed class covers investors who bought Intel shares between July 23 and October 29, 2026. It seeks unspecified damages. Intel had not publicly responded to the lawsuit in the initial reports.

## Still up for the year

Intel shares closed on Friday at $59.66. That is far below the $95 price of the August share sale and the stock's June 30 peak of $142.35, but still up 62% for the year.

The US government became one of Intel's biggest shareholders in 2025, when it took a stake of about 10% in the company.

## Chip stocks under pressure

The lawsuit adds to a difficult stretch for chip stocks. Investors are bracing for [Nvidia's results on Tuesday](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/trump-administration-name-nvidia-ai-national-champions-wsj-2026/), its first since Jensen Huang resigned as CEO, and Reuters reported that a selloff in AI stocks resumed on Monday.

Shareholder lawsuits often follow sharp stock drops, and many are dismissed or settled. To win, the plaintiffs will need to show that Intel knowingly or recklessly misled investors, not just that its forecast disappointed.
