Iran has struck at Bahrain again. Its Revolutionary Guards said they sent six drones at a US air defence radar at al-Riffa air base early on Sunday, triggering sirens across the Gulf kingdom and an order for residents to take shelter.

Bahrain says none of the drones got through. The US ambassador went further, calling Iran's claims of a successful strike false. It is the second time in three days that Iran has targeted the island, which hosts the headquarters of the US Navy's Fifth Fleet.

## Sirens before dawn

Residents reported explosions around 4am local time (01:00 GMT), followed by a second series of blasts at about 6:30am (03:30 GMT), according to AFP. Bahrain's interior ministry sent out two alerts within roughly three hours, warning of a "potential threat."

The ministry urged people to stay calm, move to the nearest safe place and follow only official channels for updates. According to Reuters, those were Bahrain's first alerts of this kind since September.

The Bahrain Defence Force said its air defence systems destroyed the Iranian attack "before they could achieve their objectives."

## What Iran claims

In a statement carried by state broadcaster IRIB and the IRGC-linked Fars news agency, the Guards said six drones from their Aerospace Force targeted the US radar at the al-Riffa helicopter base. They called the operation the "second phase" of their response to what they describe as US aggression, and said more drones were used than on Friday.

The Guards offered no evidence of damage. Their statement again declared that "the Strait of Hormuz is closed" and warned that any American action would bring "an even harsher" response. They also dismissed the US escort mission for tankers as a failure.

## US and Bahrain push back

Stephanie Hallett, the US ambassador to Bahrain, said every drone was intercepted and there was no damage and no one was hurt. "Once again, IRGC claims are false," she said, according to News of Bahrain.

US Central Command had not issued its own statement on Sunday's attack at the time of writing.

Bahrain's foreign ministry condemned "the continued brutal Iranian attacks on its territory" and said it holds Tehran "fully responsible for their consequences."

## Friday's attack

Sunday's strike came two days after the IRGC said it had destroyed a US early-warning radar in Bahrain. Iran said that operation was retaliation for an alleged US strike on a telecommunications tower in Sirik, in southern Iran's Hormozgan province.

Central Command rejected the claim within hours. "The IRGC launched two one-way attack drones toward Bahrain and both were shot down by U.S. forces. There were no injuries or damage," it said on X. Reuters reported that Friday's attack was Iran's first claimed strike on US military assets in a Gulf state since early September.

## A war fought over the strait

The attacks are the latest flashpoint in the war that began on February 28, when the United States and Israel launched strikes on Iran. Around a fifth of the world's oil and gas normally passes through the Strait of Hormuz, but shipping has been heavily restricted since.

Iran insists it should control the waterway and has attacked what it calls "unauthorised" vessels. Earlier this week, [oil tankers hit mines and caught fire near the strait](/articles/oil-tanker-burns-mine-strait-of-hormuz-iran-threatens-europe-bases/), and US officials said American forces carried out limited strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island after the IRGC fired on a US aircraft.

A preliminary US-Iran deal to end the fighting and reopen the strait collapsed within weeks of being agreed in June. This week President Donald Trump said the US no longer needed a deal with Iran, while Qatar continues to mediate between the two sides.
