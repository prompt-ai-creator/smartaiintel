> **Update, November 16:** Elon Musk has formally signed the open letter calling for Huang's return, which now has more than 165,000 signatures. Megaspeed says it has launched an internal investigation. Details below.

Jensen Huang is considering a return as Nvidia's chief executive, just days after he [resigned from the company he co-founded](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/jensen-huang-resigns-nvidia-ceo-colette-kress-harvey-jones-601b-wipeout/), the Financial Times reported on Sunday.

Huang has discussed the possibility of coming back with confidants, the newspaper said, citing people familiar with the matter. His supporters in the tech industry are also organising to push Nvidia's board to reinstate him.

The report came only hours after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the chipmaker needs [a "dramatic shakeup" and an outside CEO](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/bessent-nvidia-dramatic-shakeup-outside-ceo-board-10-days-2026/), setting up a direct clash between Huang's allies and the Trump administration.

## Update: Musk signs as letter passes 165,000

Late on Sunday, Elon Musk added his name to the open letter demanding that Nvidia's board "bring Jensen back while an independent investigation establishes the facts." It had more than 165,000 signatures by Monday, The Guardian reported.

More than 7,500 signatories identified themselves as Nvidia employees, about 18% of the company's workforce, according to the newspaper. Other names include Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney, Y Combinator president Garry Tan, Replit CEO Amjad Masad and Uber co-founder Travis Kalanick.

Huang "erred, yes – to err is to be human – but I do not believe anything he has done rises to the bar of separating him from his company," Kalanick wrote.

Not everyone thinks the campaign can work. Wedbush analyst Dan Ives told Newsweek he sees "no chance Jensen comes back to Nvidia as CEO." Corporate governance expert Charles Elson said: "Bringing him back doesn't solve any problems at all."

Meanwhile Megaspeed, the Singapore company at the centre of the scandal, said on Sunday that Alice Huang "officially left Megaspeed effective August 30, 2025, and currently has no role with the company." It said it runs a "legitimate and lawful business" and has hired outside lawyers to carry out an internal investigation, including a review of its compliance with US export controls. US Attorney General Todd Blanche has said the Justice Department has not concluded whether Alice Huang was a Chinese spy, and Beijing has rejected the allegations.

The pressure on Nvidia is also coming from the other direction. The Wall Street Journal reported on Sunday that the Trump administration plans to [name Nvidia an AI "national champion"](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/trump-administration-name-nvidia-ai-national-champions-wsj-2026/), even as Bessent demands an outside CEO.

## A Silicon Valley "war room"

One Huang ally told the FT they are putting together a "war room" of Silicon Valley executives to lobby the board and to try to win support from the White House.

"Everyone that matters in tech outside the frontier labs loves Jensen," the ally said, adding that the effort has Huang's support.

The campaign had already gone public over the weekend. Tesla and SpaceX chief Elon Musk and Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney are among the executives backing an open letter calling on the board to "Bring Jensen Back". Some Nvidia employees have signed it too, a sign of the loyalty Huang still commands inside the company. By Monday the letter had drawn more than 165,000 signatures, according to The Guardian.

Musk said on Saturday that Nvidia risks losing "critical" deals without Huang.

## Big investors want the door left open

Two prominent Nvidia shareholders have also weighed in. Altimeter Capital founder Brad Gerstner and Atreides Management's Gavin Baker wrote to the board on Friday urging it to keep "all options on the table, including the potential return of Jensen Huang."

They pointed to his record over more than three decades, in which he turned Nvidia from a graphics-card maker into the world's most valuable company and the main supplier of chips for the AI boom.

## The obstacles

A comeback would face serious hurdles.

The first is Washington. Bessent said on Fox News on Sunday that the board should "go outside the company" for its next leader, and he questioned whether Nvidia had pushed "seemingly illicit sales" to boost revenue. He wants answers from the board and its outside counsel within 10 days.

The second is the board itself. Its independent directors have [hired law firm Wachtell Lipton](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/nvidia-rebounds-wachtell-lipton-probe-trump-defends-jensen-huang/) to investigate, and that review has not reported. Bringing Huang back before it finishes would open the board to criticism that it ignored its own process.

The third is ownership. Unlike some founder-CEOs, Huang does not hold a controlling stake, so he cannot overrule the directors.

Huang stepped down on Thursday after reports about his relationship with Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, a company suspected of diverting Nvidia chips to China. US officials suspect she worked for Chinese intelligence. Megaspeed says she left the company in August 2025. The [FBI is investigating](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/fbi-probe-shareholder-lawsuit-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang/).

## The succession race

Nvidia is currently run by interim CEO Colette Kress, its long-serving chief financial officer, with Harvey C. Jones as chairman. [Here is who they are](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/who-are-colette-kress-harvey-jones-nvidia-interim-ceo-chairman/).

The FT reported on Friday that the board had [approached Arm CEO Rene Haas](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/nvidia-approaches-arm-ceo-rene-haas-succeed-jensen-huang/) as a possible successor, though the board has said no formal search process has started.

The comeback talk adds a third path: an outsider, as Bessent wants; a familiar figure such as Haas; or Huang himself.

## Earnings on Tuesday

All of this lands two days before Nvidia reports quarterly results on Tuesday, November 17. Kress will lead the call as interim CEO. Analysts are expected to ask about the leadership search, the board's investigation and the Singapore sales figures Bessent highlighted.

Neither Huang nor Nvidia had publicly commented on the FT report.
