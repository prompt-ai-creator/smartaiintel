Five people on a JetBlue flight from Puerto Rico were checked for injuries after the plane hit severe turbulence on its way to Fort Lauderdale, Florida, on Tuesday morning, officials said.

JetBlue Flight 1717 was flying from Ponce, Puerto Rico, to Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood International Airport (FLL) when it ran into the rough air. Broward Sheriff Fire Rescue said it received reports of multiple injuries on board at about 6:25 a.m. local time, more than half an hour before the plane landed.

"JetBlue Flight 1717, arriving from Ponce, Puerto Rico, reportedly encountered severe turbulence, resulting in injuries to several passengers and crew members," Battalion Chief Michael Kane told the Miami Herald.

## Five evaluated, all decline hospital

The aircraft landed safely at about 6:58 a.m. and taxied to Terminal 3, where fire rescue crews were waiting.

"Fire rescue personnel identified five individuals reporting injuries or requiring evaluation," the agency said. All five, a mix of passengers and crew members, declined to be taken to a hospital. Officials did not describe the nature of the injuries.

There were no reports of damage to the plane, and the flight did not need to divert.

## What JetBlue said

JetBlue confirmed the incident in a statement.

"JetBlue flight 1717 from Ponce, Puerto Rico (PSE), to Fort Lauderdale (FLL) experienced turbulence during the flight. The aircraft landed safely, where medical personnel evaluated customers and crewmembers for injuries. No one was transported to a hospital," the airline said.

"The safety of our customers and crewmembers remains our top priority," it added.

## Storms over the Bahamas

Meteorologists said the flight most likely ran into thunderstorms along its route.

CNN meteorologist Brandon Miller said storms were affecting Turks and Caicos and the southern Bahamas on Tuesday morning. "Other aircraft in the area have been reporting light to moderate turbulence," he said. "Storms extend up to 46,000 feet, so aircraft would be trying to fly around the strongest storms."

"There were multiple thunderstorms in the southern Bahamas across the flight path, along with a few reports of moderate turbulence in the Aviation Weather Center observations," said AccuWeather meteorologist Alexander Duffus.

FlightAware tracking data shows the flight left Ponce on time at 5:01 a.m. and reached a cruising altitude of 36,000 feet before descending to 23,900 feet over the Bahamas. The data alone does not show whether that descent was related to the turbulence.

Broward Sheriff Fire Rescue said it had no other reports of turbulence-related injuries on flights landing at Fort Lauderdale on Tuesday morning.

## Why seatbelts matter

Severe turbulence can strike with little warning. Passengers and flight attendants who are standing or not buckled in can be thrown against seats, ceilings or overhead bins, even when the plane itself is not damaged. Aviation safety officials advise travelers to keep their seatbelts fastened whenever they are seated, even when the seatbelt sign is off.

The incident comes during a busy period for US air travel, days after a [nationwide FAA ground stop](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/faa-lifts-nationwide-ground-stop-flights-resume-airspace-reopened/) and ahead of the Thanksgiving travel rush.
