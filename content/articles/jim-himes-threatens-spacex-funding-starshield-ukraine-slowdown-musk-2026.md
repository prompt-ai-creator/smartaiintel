The Democrat who will soon run the House Intelligence Committee is putting Elon Musk's government business on notice. Rep. Jim Himes of Connecticut has warned that funding for SpaceX-built government systems is at risk until Musk and the Trump administration explain who ordered slowdowns of Ukraine's military satellite service, [CNN reported](https://localnews8.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2026/11/20/top-democrat-to-trump-intel-chief-no-funding-for-musks-spacex-without-answers-on-ukraine-starshield-slowdown/) on Friday, Nov. 20.

The threat has spread widely online since then, often in viral posts saying Himes "will block" SpaceX funding. That goes further than what he has done so far. Here is what is confirmed.

## What Himes actually wrote

Himes sent two letters. The first was a classified message to Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton. "Simply, our committee cannot authorize funds for the development of government systems that the government will not fully control," he wrote, according to an excerpt obtained by CNN.

The second went to Musk, asking for a meeting. "My ask to you is straightforward. Explain the changes that were made to the Starshield service in October 2026, why those changes were made and whether those changes were made unilaterally by SpaceX or were directed by a US government official," Himes wrote.

He added a warning: "It is difficult for me to continue supporting the authorization of funding for these SpaceX-developed, government-owned systems, until I receive satisfactory answers to my questions about the Starshield service in Ukraine." He also wrote that a possible unilateral change to a government contract, combined with SpaceX's lack of response to Congress, "calls into question every contract the US government has with SpaceX."

## Can he really cut off SpaceX?

Not yet. Himes is currently the top Democrat on the committee and becomes chairman in January, when Democrats take control of Congress after the midterm elections. His most direct tool is "ring fencing," which lets the committee legally hold back money for specific projects in the annual Intelligence Authorization Act until conditions are met. He could also work with appropriators to redirect or block funding, according to CNN.

There is a catch. SpaceX holds billions of dollars in US government contracts for space systems that the military depends on, so actually withholding money could put American defense assets at risk too.

## Who slowed Starshield? Nobody has said

Starshield is the military version of Starlink, and Ukraine relies on it to fly drones and intercept Russian attacks. Ukrainian forces lost full access in mid-October, after the Trump administration threatened to withhold intelligence from Kyiv over its strikes on Russian refineries.

The blame has bounced around ever since. Some Trump officials said Musk "went rogue." SpaceX executives and defense officials privately told some lawmakers the decision originally came from the White House, a source told CNN, while Trump has repeatedly denied any direct involvement. A Pentagon official said Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth "had no idea it was happening." Musk himself posted that "any limits on speed or usage are decided by the US military and ultimately POTUS," [The Hill reported](https://thehill.com/homenews/senate/6161875-warren-musk-starshield-investigation/).

Himes is keeping both explanations open. "There is an outcome where it was actually really bad activity and contravention of a Pentagon contract or maybe (SpaceX) did something they were entitled to do but just did not give anybody any warning," he told CNN. "But we are going to get to the bottom of it."

## Musk and Zelensky trade accusations

The dispute turned personal this week. At the New York Times DealBook Summit, where Musk also [said he is not MAGA](/articles/elon-musk-not-maga-regrets-500-million-midterm-spending-dealbook-2026/), he said Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky was "untruthful" about the slowdowns. Zelensky hit back in an interview with CNN's Jim Sciutto. "When I said that Starshield doesn't work, it was 100% truth," he said, asking what reason he would have to say it did not work.

Ukraine says a second round of limits began in early November over occupied territory and along the front line. "The contact line is the first line of our defence and the first point for interception of Russian drones," Ukraine's chargé d'affaires in Washington, Denys Sienik, told [Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/senate-gearing-up-to-investigate-ukraines-access-to-starshield/). He told CNN on Friday that Kyiv was in touch with the Pentagon but had received no response from SpaceX.

## More investigations are coming

Himes is not alone. Sen. Chris Coons of Delaware, a contender to chair the Senate Foreign Relations Committee, promised a hearing on the issue. "Those are services paid for by American taxpayers under contract. This is not Elon Musk's personal toy," he said in Halifax, according to Breaking Defense.

The concern is bipartisan. Republican Sen. Thom Tillis called the October outage "at best" an "unacceptable error or lapse," [The Hill reported](https://thehill.com/homenews/senate/6150025-thom-tillis-pentagon-ig-ukraine-starlink-limits/), and a group of senators from both parties asked the Pentagon's inspector general to investigate, a request the watchdog declined. The Starshield fight has also become part of the wider peace push, which has included [talks between Zelenskyy and Trump envoys in Dublin](/articles/zelenskyy-witkoff-kushner-dublin-europe-peace-talks-pow-swap-starshield/).

CNN said it had reached out to SpaceX for comment on Himes' letters.
