President Donald Trump's plan to shut down a historic public golf course in Washington DC has been halted by the courts, the day before the gates were due to close.

US District Judge Ana Reyes issued a temporary restraining order on Saturday that stops the government from closing East Potomac Park and East Potomac Golf Links. The National Park Service had planned to close most of the park at 9pm on Sunday so work could begin on a 15-month rebuild of the course Trump has called "Trash Island."

Her order lasts until November 28 unless the court acts sooner.

## "Pretextual," the judge says

The government argued that the closure was about safety and staffing: a crumbling seawall around the park, and the end of the lease with the course operator, National Links Trust, on Sunday.

Reyes did not accept either argument. "The Court, based on the current record, readily finds that these explanations are pretextual," she wrote in a 30-page decision. She said the government had likely broken at least two federal laws, the National Environmental Policy Act and the Administrative Procedure Act, "to meet an arbitrary deadline."

On the seawall, she noted that the Park Service has considered the whole perimeter a hazard since 2019 and had previously fenced off only the dangerous sections. Three days before deciding on the closure, she added, it let more than 30,000 people run a marathon along that same route.

"The government proposes to close a historic park, daily enjoyed by Washingtonians of all stripes, indefinitely," Reyes wrote. She said this would cause irreparable harm to the plaintiffs, who use the park regularly.

## Trump's own posts worked against him

Trump's social media posts weighed heavily in the ruling. On October 26 he wrote on Truth Social: "We are closing the East Potomac Golf Course Site as scheduled on November 15th." He linked that date to a rebuild into a "World Championship Public Facility" capable of hosting events such as the US Open and the Ryder Cup.

Reyes said those posts suggested the redesign plan was final and that the park was being closed to carry it out. "Both points favor granting Plaintiffs' motion for another temporary restraining order," she wrote.

## What the order means

The ruling keeps the course and the rest of the park south of Buckeye Drive SW, including Ohio Drive SW, open during the hours they were open on November 14. That covers Hains Point, waterfront paths, fishing spots and the miniature golf course.

It does not force the administration to actively manage the park or keep golf running, and it remains unclear whether regular golf services will continue now that National Links Trust has left. Officials can still carry out seawall repairs and make limited closures for imminent safety threats, as long as they tell the court and the plaintiffs within 24 hours.

## A long and bitter fight

The legal battle began in February, when the DC Preservation League and two local golfers sued over the redevelopment. It escalated in October, when the Park Service cut down hundreds of trees at the course over a holiday weekend with very little notice to the court. The plaintiffs counted 529 trees removed; the administration put the figure at 430.

Reyes then ordered a halt to tree cutting. The Justice Department asked her to step aside over what it called her "egregious behavior," a request she rejected. She has also appointed former US Attorney Jessie Liu as a special master to examine whether government lawyers misled the court. The department objects to that appointment.

The coalition behind the lawsuit, which includes Democracy Forward and the Democracy Defenders Fund, said it was "grateful that the court has stopped the government from unlawfully closing East Potomac Park." The BBC said it had contacted the White House for comment.

The 106-year-old course sits on Hains Point, about two miles south of the White House, and is one of three public golf courses the National Park Service runs in the capital.
