New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani said on Monday that he is disbanding the New York Police Department's Strategic Response Group, the unit that has handled both counterterrorism and mass protests for more than a decade.

Police Commissioner Jessica Tisch backed the move. The two leaders announced it together after months of talks. It delivers on one of the best-known promises from Mamdani's 2025 campaign.

"During our campaign, I promised New Yorkers that we would disband the Strategic Response Group and separate counterterrorism from protest response, and today we are delivering on that promise," Mamdani said in a statement. "New Yorkers exercising their constitutional right to protest should not be met with the same police response as a terrorism threat."

## What replaces the SRG

The unit's work will be split up rather than simply cut.

- **Counterterrorism:** heavy-weapons deployments at critical infrastructure and other counterterror duties move to the NYPD's Intelligence and Counterterrorism Bureau.
- **Protests:** a new Community Dialogue and De-escalation Division will sit inside the Mayor's Office of Community Safety, which Mamdani created in March. It will be civilian-led and will have no weapons or arrest powers. Its staff will talk to organizers before and during demonstrations.
- **Police support at protests:** the NYPD will set up a First Amendment Support Team, known as FAST, made up of 30 uniformed officers trained in communication and de-escalation.

Tisch told ABC7 New York that the SRG's roughly 500 officers will be reassigned, many of them to counterterrorism work. City Hall said officers will be vetted, with complaints to the Civilian Complaint Review Board and the NYPD's Internal Affairs Bureau taken into account.

The city says the overhaul will not raise or cut NYPD headcount and will cost nothing extra. ABC7 reported that the unit is due to be dissolved in spring 2027, with civilians hired and trained over the next year. The NYPD keeps its power to make arrests at demonstrations.

## New limits on crowd-control tactics

The plan also sets rules for how police can manage protests. According to NBC New York, officers will not be allowed to use pepper spray as a crowd-management tool, and long-range acoustic devices must stay below 90 decibels. amNY reported that the plan also formalizes limits on tear gas, long guns and kettling, the tactic of surrounding protesters so they cannot leave.

## Tisch: 'I would never compromise on security'

Tisch kept her job when Mamdani took office, and she had publicly defended the unit. At a City Council hearing in March she said her talks with the mayor were about how the city polices protests in general, "not to disband" the SRG. She also said the unit was available at only 245 of the 4,255 protests the NYPD handled in 2025, about 6%.

On Monday she said the new structure makes the department stronger.

"I would never support changes that diminish the NYPD's ability to protect New Yorkers, or that put our officers in a position where they lack the resources necessary to confront a serious threat," Tisch said. "These changes will make us a stronger, more effective department."

Asked by ABC7 whether the deal was a compromise, she said: "No, I would never compromise on security for New Yorkers."

## Why the SRG was so controversial

The SRG was formed in 2015 under then-Commissioner Bill Bratton, first to respond to terror threats. Over time it was sent to protests, parades and major incidents as well.

Its role in the 2020 demonstrations after the police killing of George Floyd drew accusations of excessive force. The city's Department of Investigation later found the NYPD had used excessive force, and a 2023 legal settlement now governs how the department polices protests. The New York Civil Liberties Union has called for the unit to be disbanded since 2021.

Police unions and other supporters say the unit's training makes it the best tool for violent or chaotic demonstrations.

## Political pressure from the left

The announcement follows months of pressure from Mamdani's left-wing allies. After protests over an ICE shooting in the Bronx neighborhood of Marble Hill in October, the city's Democratic Socialists of America chapter and 17 socialist state lawmakers and nominees demanded that Tisch disband the unit or be removed. Mamdani stood by his commissioner.

Council Member Chi Ossé, a longtime critic of the unit and sponsor of a bill to dismantle it, appeared with Mamdani in a video announcing the change. It is not yet clear whether his bill will be rewritten to match the new plan.
