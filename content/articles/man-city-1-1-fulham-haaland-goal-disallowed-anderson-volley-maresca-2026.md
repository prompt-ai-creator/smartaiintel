Manchester City's slump goes on. Enzo Maresca's side were held **1-1 by Fulham** at the Etihad Stadium on Saturday, leaving City without a win in four Premier League games after a record seven-game winning start to the season.

Rodrigo Muniz gave Fulham a shock 16th-minute lead, Erling Haaland had an equalizer ruled out for a foul on goalkeeper Bernd Leno, and Elliot Anderson's second-half volley rescued a point for the hosts.

## Muniz punishes a flat City

Fulham struck first on the counterattack. Former City winger Oscar Bobb drove forward down the right and picked out Muniz, who curled a right-footed shot into the top corner past Gianluigi Donnarumma, according to [BBC Sport's live coverage](https://www.bbc.com/sport/football/live/c3kgdxnggq7pt).

City dominated the ball but looked slow. Home fans whistled and jeered as the first half wore on, and David Affengruber made a string of blocks at the back for Fulham. Haaland missed two clear chances before the break.

## Why Haaland's goal was disallowed

The biggest talking point came around the 25-minute mark. Haaland met an Iliman Ndiaye cross, Leno parried it, and Haaland bundled the loose ball into the net. Referee Tom Bramall gave a free kick to Fulham instead.

The Premier League backed the call. "The referee's call of a free-kick to Fulham and no goal to Manchester City was checked and confirmed by VAR, with the goalkeeper deemed to be in control of the ball," its Match Centre said, according to the [Manchester Evening News](https://www.manchestereveningnews.co.uk/sport/football/football-news/premier-league-explain-erling-haaland-34947517). The VAR explanation added: "The ball is in control between both hands and then is played out of that possession by the attacker, and then into the back of the net."

Haaland and Maresca both looked baffled, and replays left room for argument over whether Leno had full control when Haaland first touched the ball.

## Anderson's volley saves a point

City finally broke through in the 57th minute. A headed clearance from a corner dropped to Anderson on the edge of the box, and he met it first time with a right-footed volley that flew through a crowd of players and past Leno. "That was an outstanding volley from Anderson, who watched the ball closely as it dropped his way," [The Guardian's live blog](https://www.theguardian.com/football/live/2026/nov/21/manchester-city-v-fulham-premier-league-live) said.

The game opened up after that. Donnarumma made two big saves from Muniz and Calvin Bassey, and Muniz headed a free chance wide. City sent on Rayan Cherki, Josko Gvardiol and Enzo Fernández but could not find a winner, and Fulham nearly snatched it late when Sander Berge pulled the ball across goal with nobody there to finish.

## What it means for Maresca and City

City won their first seven league games under Maresca, then lost to Aston Villa, Brighton and Nottingham Forest before the international break. It was their first run of three straight league defeats since 2004, [The National reported](https://www.thenationalnews.com/sport/football/2026/11/09/enzo-maresca-aims-to-kick-start-manchester-city-season-after-run-of-defeats/). Before kickoff they were third, six points behind leaders Arsenal.

Maresca had said there was "no need to panic" and rejected claims that the club's pending appeal against more than 100 Premier League financial charges was affecting his players. "We are conceding too many goals. Most of them are avoidable," he said on Friday, [Reuters reported](https://www.straitstimes.com/sport/football/maresca-shrugs-off-links-between-man-citys-slump-and-financial-case). He has also faced questions about the balance of a midfield built around summer signings Fernández, Anderson and Ayyoub Bouaddi, [The Guardian reported](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2026/nov/20/premier-league-news-everton-jack-grealish-manchester-city-liverpool-chelsea).

Defender Marc Guehi, who [missed England's Nations League games](/articles/england-guehi-burn-squad/) with what Maresca called a "small issue," was available again.

For Fulham, the point ended a run of 20 straight defeats to City in all competitions. They have not beaten City since 2009.

City host Napoli in the Champions League this week before a trip to Arsenal next weekend that could decide whether they stay in the title race. Elsewhere in the Premier League, Manchester United's managerial change has dominated the season, with [Mauricio Pochettino now in charge at Old Trafford](/articles/mauricio-pochettino-tearful-press-conference-official-manchester-united-manager/).
