Manchester United will convene a decisive leadership summit in Monaco on Wednesday where minority owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe, chief executive Omar Berrada, and technical director Jason Wilcox are set to determine whether Michael Carrick remains head coach beyond the November international break. According to [BBC Sport reporting](https://www.bbc.com/sport/football/articles/cmy0r041ed0po), Carrick enters those talks in acute jeopardy after Sunday's chaotic four apiece draw against Aston Villa left United 18th in the Premier League table with one victory from their opening 10 league fixtures.

That return represents Manchester United's worst top flight start since the 1986 to 1987 campaign, the autumn in which Ron Atkinson was dismissed and replaced by Alex Ferguson. Across all competitions this term, Carrick's side have managed just three wins from 15 matches while conceding 19 goals in 10 league outings, forcing Ratcliffe's football leadership group to weigh an immediate change in the dugout before domestic play resumes later this month.

While club executives insisted on Tuesday morning that no final verdict will be rubber stamped until Ratcliffe, Berrada, and Wilcox sit down face to face in Monaco, [Sky Sports reported](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/13610412/michael-carrick-future-man-utd-head-coach-position-increasingly-in-doubt-but-no-final-decision-made) that United States men's national team head coach Mauricio Pochettino remains open to holding formal discussions if Old Trafford chiefs pull the trigger.

## How a promising permanent appointment unraveled by November

Carrick initially took charge at Old Trafford in January and steadied a turbulent dressing room well enough to earn the permanent role in the summer. Early signs in August hinted that his calm touchline demeanor might carry into the new campaign, highlighted by an emphatic 5 to 2 victory over Ipswich Town featuring a Bruno Fernandes hat trick alongside cup progress past Hull City.

Those bright opening weeks quickly gave way to defensive fragility, muscle injuries, and public friction inside the squad. League setbacks against Everton, Manchester City, Fulham, Tottenham Hotspur, Bournemouth, and Chelsea dragged United into the relegation places, compounded by an EFL Cup exit against Brighton and Hove Albion that stripped away a domestic trophy route before November.

European nights deepened the sense of drift rather than offering relief. In the Champions League, United conceded inside the opening minute of a 2 to 0 loss to Atletico Madrid, after which Argentina defender Lisandro Martinez publicly criticized the club's summer recruitment and aimed pointed remarks at technical director Wilcox. Days later, Italian side Como struck inside the first two minutes on their way to handing United a humiliating 4 to 1 Champions League defeat.

Captain Bruno Fernandes followed that loss to Como by questioning Carrick's tactical structure in post match interviews. Carrick responded by dropping Fernandes from the starting lineup against Bournemouth before restoring the Portuguese midfielder and his armband for the trip to Stamford Bridge, only for United to lose both matches without resolving the underlying gaps between midfield and defense.

Injuries to striker Benjamin Sesko and left back Luke Shaw further destabilized selection, while forward Marcus Rashford has not featured since the fallout around the Atletico Madrid defeat. Forced into constant back four improvisation, Carrick has already started five different players at left back this season, leaving United vulnerable to wide overloads and late second phase deliveries.

Sunday's wild stalemate against Aston Villa at Old Trafford captured every contradiction of Carrick's tenure in miniature. Played against a backdrop of supporters carrying green and gold protest balloons in the stands, United showed enough attacking urgency to score four times yet repeatedly surrendered soft territory and central runners to let leads slip, leaving the club stranded in 18th place as players dispersed for the [November international window](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/england-guehi-burn-squad/).

## Pochettino buyout terms and rival contenders on United's shortlist

Should the Monaco summit conclude that Carrick's position is untenable, Manchester United face a complex financial and diplomatic landscape to secure an elite replacement midseason. Pochettino, long admired by figures inside the INEOS sporting hierarchy and previously interviewed before United took different paths in past cycles, is currently preparing the United States squad for a two legged Concacaf Nations League play off tie against Haiti on November 14 and November 18.

Extracting the Argentine tactician from his US Soccer contract carries a buyout clause estimated at roughly seven million pounds. Timing would also require delicate coordination with American federation officials given the immediacy of Friday's first leg against Haiti, though Pochettino's camp has signaled willingness to listen if United table a firm offer after Wednesday's executive meeting.

Three additional high profile names sit alongside Pochettino in boardroom discussions: Newcastle United manager Eddie Howe, Germany national team boss Julian Nagelsmann, and former Chelsea, Inter Milan, and Napoli title winner Antonio Conte. Each candidate presents distinct trade offs in style, compensation cost, and availability during a congested winter calendar.

Howe commands strong domestic admiration from Wilcox and Ratcliffe for transforming Newcastle's athletic intensity and recruitment value, yet prying him out of St James' Park midseason would demand substantial compensation and protracted club to club negotiations. Nagelsmann offers progressive pressing principles aligned with modern sporting director models, though international commitments complicate an instant November arrival. Conte brings a proven track record of rapid Premier League turnarounds, having lifted the league title in his debut season at Chelsea, but his combative approach to transfer authority has historically clashed with multi layered executive structures like the Berrada and Wilcox partnership.

## Financial pressure and what awaits after the international break

Beyond compensation fees for Carrick's coaching staff and any incoming boss, Ratcliffe's Monaco deliberations carry heavy balance sheet stakes. Missing European qualification entirely from a bottom half finish would punch a nine figure hole in projected broadcast and matchday revenue at the exact moment United are navigating Premier League profitability and sustainability thresholds.

At the same time, club insiders acknowledge that waiting until December risks allowing the gap to the top half to widen beyond recovery before the festive fixture pileup. Twenty eight points still separate November anxiety from May survival battles, yet only four clubs in Premier League history have opened a 10 match slate with a single victory and gone on to finish inside the top eight.

Ratcliffe, Berrada, and Wilcox must therefore decide in Monaco whether Carrick deserves time for injured regulars like Shaw and Sesko to return after the international break or whether dressing room authority has eroded past repair following the public criticisms from Martinez and Fernandes. With supporters restless and United occupying the final relegation spot for two straight weeks, Wednesday's Riviera gathering is poised to define Old Trafford's season.
