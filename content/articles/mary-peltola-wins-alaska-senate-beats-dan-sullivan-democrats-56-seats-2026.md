Democrat Mary Peltola has won Alaska's US Senate race, defeating two-term Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan and completing a political comeback in a state President Donald Trump won by 13 points in 2024.

The Associated Press called the race on Tuesday after an update in the vote count pushed Peltola's share above 50%. That is the threshold needed to win outright and skip Alaska's ranked choice voting tally. In the latest count, Peltola had 144,302 votes, or 50.12%, to Sullivan's 131,844, according to Alaska's News Source.

Alaska was the last Senate race of the 2026 midterms to be decided. Peltola's victory gives Democrats a **56-44 majority** in the next Senate. Republicans went into the election with a 53-47 majority. Democrats flipped nine Republican-held seats, the most since 1958, including [Scott Colom's upset of Cindy Hyde-Smith in Mississippi](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/scott-colom-unseats-cindy-hyde-smith-mississippi-senate-55-democrat-majority/).

## 'A monumental step forward'

"Over the course of this campaign I've spoken with elders in tears about empty fish racks; families struggling to find housing they can afford; and parents in the Bush faced with the impossible choice of heating their homes or putting food on the table," Peltola said. "Today we take a monumental step forward in addressing those issues and putting Alaska first."

She said voters had sent a clear message that "the status quo in D.C. is not working for Alaska."

Sullivan's campaign said before the race was called that it would not comment until all ballots were counted.

## Why the AP called the race now

Only a small number of ballots remain to be counted, most of them absentee mail ballots, according to the AP. Peltola has easily beaten Sullivan among those late ballots, suggesting her share will stay above 50%.

Even if her share fell back below 50% and triggered a ranked choice count, the AP said, ballot data released by state officials shows she would still win comfortably. Most voters who did not rank Peltola or Sullivan first either ranked no second choice or did not overwhelmingly favor Sullivan.

## A history-making win

Peltola, who is Yup'ik and grew up in western Alaska, will be the **first Alaska Native to serve in the US Senate**, NPR reported. She is the first Democrat to win a Senate race in Alaska since 2008, and only the fifth Democrat to represent the state in the Senate since statehood. The most recent was Mark Begich, whom Sullivan defeated in 2014.

She is from Bethel, a western Alaska hub of about 7,000 people that is not connected to the state's road system. She previously served in the US House.

Peltola finished first in the August primary with 49.5% of the vote, ahead of Sullivan's 41.4%.

## The 'second Dan Sullivan' controversy

The race was shaken in its final week. The New York Times reported allegations that members of Peltola's campaign helped recruit a retired teacher named Dan J. Sullivan to run, in order to draw votes away from the incumbent. Trump called for Peltola to be prosecuted.

Peltola denied any involvement. "I knew nothing about any other candidates on the ballot except for me and the incumbent," she told supporters days before the election. Dan J. Sullivan has also denied any contact with her campaign.

The Justice Department filed a lawsuit, which Trump said he ordered, seeking to block certification of the result if Sullivan lost. A federal judge dismissed the case, finding such an order would "clearly violate" Alaskans' right to a secret ballot. The department is appealing and has confirmed a criminal investigation, without saying what potential crimes are being examined. Alaska's governor has also ordered an investigation.

"Alaskan voters deserve a fair election, not to be misled in an attempt to rig outcomes," the Justice Department said.

Preliminary results suggest the other candidates did not act as spoilers. Peltola's margin over Sullivan was more than double the votes won by Dan J. Sullivan.

## Affordability at the center of her campaign

Peltola ran on the cost of living, a powerful issue in rural Alaska, where groceries and fuel can cost two or three times as much as elsewhere. She revived her "fish, family and freedom" slogan and sought to distance herself from national Democrats.

"Alaskans who know me know that I'm not liberal or radical," she said during the campaign.

Trump and Vice President JD Vance both traveled to Alaska in the final month to campaign for Sullivan. Alaska's US House race, between Republican Rep. Nick Begich and independent Bill Hill, has not yet been called.
