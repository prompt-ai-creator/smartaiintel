Less than 24 hours after [co-founder Jensen Huang resigned](/articles/jensen-huang-resigns-nvidia-ceo-colette-kress-harvey-jones-601b-wipeout/) from the chipmaker he built over 33 years, Nvidia's board of directors has made its first high-profile external move to fill the most powerful executive seat in the global artificial intelligence industry.

Nvidia's board has approached Arm Holdings chief executive Rene Haas as a potential candidate to become the company's next permanent CEO, the Financial Times reported Friday afternoon, citing people familiar with the succession process. Reuters noted that Haas is one of several contenders under early review and that there is no guarantee Nvidia will extend a formal offer or that Haas would agree to depart the British semiconductor architecture firm.

Both Arm Holdings and Nvidia declined to comment publicly on the outreach Friday as [interim chief executive Colette Kress and newly named board chair Harvey C. Jones](/articles/who-are-colette-kress-harvey-jones-nvidia-interim-ceo-chairman/) convened Nvidia's senior leadership team at its Santa Clara headquarters ahead of Tuesday's fiscal third-quarter earnings report. U.S.-listed shares of Arm pared deeper morning declines following the report to trade down roughly 5.5%, while Nvidia shares traded down between 3% and 4% amid a [broader Friday semiconductor selloff](/articles/nasdaq-tumbles-nvidia-reverses-lower-chip-selloff-traps-dip-buyers/).

## Why Rene Haas stands out on Nvidia's succession radar

Among external semiconductor chiefs, few executives know Nvidia's internal engineering culture, data-center architecture, and customer ecosystem as intimately as Haas. Before joining Cambridge-based Arm in 2013, the American-born executive spent seven formative years at Nvidia between 2006 and 2013, rising to vice president and general manager of its computing products business.

After moving to Arm to lead its intellectual property licensing groups, Haas was elevated to chief executive officer in February 2022—the exact day Nvidia and Arm parent SoftBank Group abandoned Nvidia's proposed $40 billion acquisition of Arm following fierce antitrust resistance from regulators in Washington, London, and Brussels.

Since taking the helm at Arm, Haas has executed one of the semiconductor sector's sharpest strategic pivots:

- In September 2023, Haas steered Arm back onto the Nasdaq in a blockbuster $54.5 billion initial public offering—the largest tech listing of that year—before driving its market value past $130 billion on surging AI data-center demand.
- He overhauled Arm's decades-old smartphone royalty model around its higher-margin Armv9 architecture and expanded compute subsystems directly into cloud servers, automotive chips, and AI PCs.
- Crucially for Nvidia customers, Haas maintained a deep technical partnership with Santa Clara: Nvidia's flagship Grace Blackwell GB200 and upcoming Vera Rubin AI superchips pair Nvidia GPUs directly with custom Arm-based central processing units.

## Obstacles to poaching Arm's chief executive

Recruiting Haas away from Cambridge and SoftBank founder Masayoshi Son would require navigating complex corporate and financial hurdles. Japanese investment conglomerate SoftBank still controls roughly 88% to 90% of Arm Holdings and has placed Haas at the center of Son's multi-hundred-billion-dollar AI infrastructure ambitions, holding a seat on SoftBank's board of directors as well.

Analysts also note that Nvidia's board—led for the first time by 1993 founding-era director Harvey C. Jones—explicitly committed Thursday to evaluating both internal and external candidates. Inside Nvidia, veteran lieutenants including executive vice president of worldwide field operations Jay Puri, accelerated computing chief Ian Buck, operations chief Debora Shoquist, and interim CEO Colette Kress hold decades of institutional continuity across Nvidia's TSMC foundry allocations and hyperscaler contracts.

External speculation earlier this week also floated AMD chief executive Lisa Su, though semiconductor analysts widely view poaching Nvidia's closest direct GPU rival as far more fraught with non-compete and antitrust friction than approaching a former Nvidia vice president whose company licenses complementary CPU designs.

## What Wall Street is watching before Tuesday's earnings call

For institutional investors rattled by Nvidia's 14% stock slide over the past five trading sessions, Friday's revelation signals that Jones and Nvidia's board are moving rapidly rather than allowing a prolonged leadership vacuum to overhang the company's 2027 Rubin chip transition.

Kress is scheduled to lead Nvidia's fiscal third-quarter 2027 earnings call after the closing bell on Tuesday, November 17, where analysts are certain to press management on the timeline and criteria for the permanent CEO search—even as [Huang prepares to testify under oath before Congress](/articles/nvidia-reclaims-4-trillion-close-third-jensen-huang-affair-paul-weiss/) ahead of Thanksgiving over the national security questions surrounding his departure.
