Nvidia blew past Wall Street's forecasts on Tuesday in its first earnings report since Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive. Revenue more than doubled, and the company forecast an even bigger quarter ahead.

The chipmaker reported revenue of $115.8 billion for its fiscal third quarter, which ended Oct. 25. That is up 103% from $57.0 billion a year earlier and 20% from the previous quarter. Analysts had expected about $109.15 billion, according to Bloomberg consensus estimates cited by Yahoo Finance.

Nvidia shares rose about 3.6% in early after-hours trading, wiping out the day's losses, according to TheStreet.

## Update: Board rules out Huang's return; shares jump after hours

*Updated Nov. 18, 2026.* Nvidia's board used the earnings call to shut the door on a comeback by Jensen Huang, hours after the former CEO said he would be "honored" to return if the board asked him.

"The Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO," board chair Harvey Jones said at the start of the call. "While this was an unexpected transition, the board acted deliberately and in the best interest of the company's employees, customers, partners and shareholders."

Jones said Huang's resignation followed the "disclosure of personal behavior inconsistent with the company's code of conduct, standards and cultural norms." Nvidia said it would not take questions about the circumstances of his exit.

Earlier on Tuesday, Huang said in his first statement since leaving that he would return, citing an "outpouring of calls from Nvidia's employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO."

Nvidia also raised its revenue growth forecast for next fiscal year by 5 points to about 75%, according to CRN. "From a business perspective, it's business as usual," Kress said. She closed the call by telling analysts: "We've got this."

Nvidia shares closed Tuesday's regular session down 0.44% at $155.46, then rose 4.82% in after-hours trading to $162.95, according to Benzinga. The stock briefly dipped after Jones' comments before climbing as the call went on. It remains about 30% below its October record.

The company still faces other questions. The Wall Street Journal has reported that the Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating Nvidia, including whether its disclosures misled investors, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has said he wants answers from the board within 10 days.

## Nvidia Q3 results vs. expectations

| Measure | Nvidia Q3 result | Wall Street estimate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue | $115.8 billion | About $109.15 billion |
| Adjusted earnings per share | $2.68 | About $2.47 |
| Data center revenue | $107.6 billion | About $101.8 billion |
| Q4 revenue outlook | $130 billion, plus or minus 3% | About $125 billion |

Adjusted earnings came to $2.68 per share, up 111% from a year ago. On a GAAP basis, Nvidia earned $2.70 per share, and net income rose 105% to $65.3 billion. Gross margin was 74.8%, slightly above the company's August guidance of about 74%.

## $130 billion outlook tops forecasts

The outlook may matter most to investors. Nvidia said it expects fourth-quarter revenue of $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3%. Analysts had been looking for about $125 billion, though some on Wall Street had said a $130 billion guide was the number the stock needed.

The fourth quarter includes an extra week. Nvidia said it is not counting on any data center computing revenue from China, and expects gross margins between 71% and 72% as memory costs rise.

## Kress: 'Our momentum is real'

The report is the first under Colette Kress, Nvidia's chief financial officer since 2013, who became [interim CEO after Huang's resignation](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/jensen-huang-resigns-nvidia-ceo-colette-kress-harvey-jones-601b-wipeout/) on Nov. 12.

"Our momentum is real. NVIDIA is an exceptional technology leader, powered by the relentless innovation of our people," Kress said. "Vera Rubin is off to an explosive start, our roadmap is on track and our ambition has never been greater."

## Data center sales jump 110%

Nvidia's data center business, which sells the chips that power artificial intelligence, brought in $107.6 billion. That is up 110% from a year ago and 21% from the previous quarter.

The company said it began shipping its new Vera Rubin NVL72 systems, calling it the fastest product ramp in its history. Racks are running at partners including CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure and Nebius. Nvidia also said it expanded Anthropic's access to its Grace Blackwell and Vera Rubin capacity through cloud providers, days before [Anthropic's planned stock market debut](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/anthropic-ipo-six-times-oversubscribed-90-billion-raise-nvidia-anchor/).

Edge computing revenue rose 42% from a year ago to $8.2 billion.

## $28 billion returned to shareholders

Nvidia returned $28.1 billion to shareholders in the quarter through share buybacks and dividends. It had about $227.3 billion left under its buyback plan after the board added $150 billion in September. The next quarterly dividend of 25 cents per share will be paid on Dec. 23 to shareholders of record on Dec. 2.

## A month of turmoil for Nvidia

The strong numbers come after one of the most turbulent months in Nvidia's history. The stock had fallen about 30% from its October high amid Huang's exit, questions over [who will lead the company](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/who-are-colette-kress-harvey-jones-nvidia-interim-ceo-chairman/) and a broader [selloff in AI and chip stocks](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/nasdaq-tumbles-nvidia-reverses-lower-chip-selloff-traps-dip-buyers/).

The quarter itself ended before Huang resigned, so investors were watching the outlook as the first forecast of the post-Huang Nvidia. Huang is due to [appear before three House committees on Dec. 1](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/jensen-huang-agrees-testify-congress-before-thanksgiving-trump-tim-cook-nvidia/), and Nvidia's board is searching for a permanent CEO.

Kress leads Nvidia's conference call with analysts at 5 p.m. ET.
