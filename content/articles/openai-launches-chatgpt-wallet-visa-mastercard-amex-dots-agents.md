Artificial intelligence assistants have spent the past two years recommending laptops, flights, and household gear—only to hand shoppers a web link and force them to type in a 16-digit credit card number themselves. On Friday, OpenAI moved to close that final mile of e-commerce.

OpenAI officially launched ChatGPT Wallet on Friday, a built-in payment system created in partnership with American Express, Mastercard, and Visa that enables its personal AI agents—known as "dots"—to fill carts and complete online checkouts directly on a user's behalf. First introduced at OpenAI's developer conference in late September, dots operate as persistent, always-on personal agents inside ChatGPT.

The feature marks OpenAI's most aggressive push yet into what Silicon Valley and Wall Street call "agentic commerce," arriving during a fierce week of AI platform competition alongside [Google's launch of Gemini 4 Hydrogen for autonomous workflows](/articles/google-launches-gemini-4-hydrogen-search-api-coding-agents/) and [Anthropic's six-times-oversubscribed $90.4 billion Nasdaq IPO](/articles/anthropic-ipo-six-times-oversubscribed-90-billion-raise-nvidia-anchor/).

## How tokenized checkout works without exposing card numbers

Consumer reluctance to hand live credit card credentials to an autonomous software bot has been one of the biggest barriers to AI shopping. OpenAI engineered ChatGPT Wallet around card-network tokenization so that neither the AI dot nor the online retailer ever sees the numbers printed on a user's physical card.

Once an eligible subscriber saves a card inside ChatGPT Wallet, each shopping request follows a strict multi-step guardrail:

- When a user instructs their dot to purchase an item, the agent assembles the order and presents a confirmation screen displaying the exact merchandise, total price, shipping details, and selected card.
- The shopper must explicitly approve every single transaction using device-level biometric security—such as Apple Face ID, Touch ID, or an Android fingerprint scan—which generates a verifiable authorization record.
- Upon biometric approval, Visa, Mastercard, or American Express issues a one-time payment token containing a transaction-specific card number, expiration date, and security code scoped solely to that purchase.
- Because the payment routes over standard card-network rails, shoppers keep all of their existing credit card points, cash-back rewards, and issuer chargeback protections if an item arrives damaged or fails to ship.

To prevent automated retail anti-bot filters from mistaking a dot for a scalper script or fraudulent checkout bot, OpenAI simultaneously published technical guidance for online merchants and partnered with payment processors Stripe and Adyen alongside fraud-detection specialists Forter and Signifyd.

## Who gets access first—and why OpenAI is charging zero fees

Rather than opening AI checkouts to hundreds of millions of free accounts overnight, OpenAI is starting inside a tightly controlled sandbox. At launch on Friday, ChatGPT Wallet is available exclusively to a limited cohort of U.S. subscribers on OpenAI's top-tier ChatGPT Pro plan who are at least 18 years old and hold an eligible U.S.-issued Visa, Mastercard, or American Express card, including supported Chase primary cards. Virtual cards, regular non-dot chats, Plus and Free tiers, and international markets are not yet supported.

"It just makes sense to watch it, start it in a contained environment, and then keep rolling from there," American Express executive Luke Gebb told CNBC on Friday, noting that OpenAI's earlier "Instant Checkout" experiment last autumn struggled to gain broad merchant traction before the company rebuilt its shopping architecture around standard merchant checkouts.

Notably, OpenAI confirmed it is taking zero cut of transactions processed through ChatGPT Wallet at launch. By waiving referral or payment fees initially, OpenAI aims to win over major retail brands ahead of the holiday shopping surge before eventually monetizing agent-driven commerce or expanding into pre-approved autonomous rules—where users will be able to set dollar caps, merchant categories, and time windows for their dots to handle routine reorders automatically.
