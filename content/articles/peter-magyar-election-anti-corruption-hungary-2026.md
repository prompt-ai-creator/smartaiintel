**Péter Magyar** won Hungary's April 2026 election in a landslide, unseating **Viktor Orbán** after 16 years in power and ushering in a new era of Hungarian politics.

## The election: A historic upset

Magyar, a relatively unknown figure nationally before the campaign, ran on a sweeping **anti-corruption platform** that resonated with Hungarian voters frustrated by years of alleged graft and cronyism under Orbán. His victory marked the first change in Hungarian leadership since Orbán returned to power in 2010.

The election was widely seen as a referendum on Orbán's 16-year rule, during which Hungary shifted toward what the European Union characterised as an "**electoral autocracy**." Critics cited the erosion of judicial independence, media freedom, and electoral fairness during Orbán's tenure.

## "End of the mafia"

Magyar's central campaign promise was to end what he called the "**mafia**" of Orbán-era governance. Since taking office, he has moved quickly to deliver on that promise:

- **Miklós Seszták**, former minister of national development under Orbán, arrested on bribery charges involving the state transport company
- **György Matolcsy**, former central bank chief, arrested on separate corruption accusations
- **Nearly $20 million in cash** uncovered hidden in farm buildings, safes, and forests during NVVH probes
- **Orbán's brother and three others** detained in a people-smuggling case

The NVVH (National Asset Recovery and Protection Office) has dubbed its corruption investigation "**Operation Purgatory**," explicitly targeting corruption from the Orbán era.

## Orbán's record and the EU's concerns

During his 16 years in power, Orbán transformed Hungary's political landscape. His government:

- Consolidated control over state media and the judiciary
- Changed electoral laws to favor Fidesz, his party
- Faced repeated EU sanctions over rule-of-law concerns
- Was labelled an "electoral autocracy" by European Parliament resolutions

Orbán has strongly denied all corruption allegations, calling them politically motivated attacks by his opponents.

## What happens next

Magyar's government faces its first major test: delivering on anti-corruption promises while maintaining political stability. The $20 million cash discovery and ongoing arrests suggest the investigation will continue to expand.

Key questions remain:
- Will more Orbán-era figures face charges?
- Can Magyar's government implement structural reforms to prevent future corruption?
- How will Hungary's relationship with the EU change under Magyar's leadership?
