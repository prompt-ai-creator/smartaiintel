# Philippines Wins First Eurovision Asia as Carmelle Takes Crown With Sobra-Sobra

The first Eurovision Song Contest ever staged in Asia has a Filipino champion. Carmelle, a 19-year-old singer and the youngest performer in the field, won Saturday night's grand final in Bangkok with Sobra-Sobra, an upbeat women's empowerment anthem that blends pop with a rap section. She finished on 211 points, 41 clear of host nation Thailand.

## A win with both the juries and the fans

Carmelle's victory was decisive because it came from both halves of the vote. The national juries gave her 93 points, the most of any act, and viewers added 118, again the highest score. No other performer topped either table.

That matters because the two votes told different stories further down the scoreboard. Thailand's duo MANG x DAOU, singing the romantic pop number Aroma, ranked only fourth with the juries on 62 points. The public lifted them to second overall: their 108 points from viewers were the second-best audience score of the night, giving a total of 170. Vietnam's Tóc Tiên, who opened the show with Em Tiên, went the other way: the juries had her second on 83, and she finished third overall on 164.

Malaysia came fourth with 152 points, followed by Laos (139), Bhutan (126), South Korea (113), Mongolia (94), Nepal (69), Cambodia (61) and Bangladesh (35).

## Tuk-tuks, beatboxers and a technical hitch

The show leaned into its host city. Each of the 11 acts arrived on stage aboard a tuk-tuk, waving their national flag as the crowd at the One Bangkok Forum cheered them in.

Some of the night's most talked-about moments came from outside the top three. Bangladesh's entry, the beatbox group Beatmosphere, performed without any backing track, building drums and bass entirely with their voices. Laos's Thinla sang Bae dressed in a traditional Lao hilltribe outfit, and her song got an unplanned encore: an apparent technical problem meant her music video played during the broadcast, so it was the last song viewers heard before voting opened. She finished fifth.

Bulgaria's Dara, who won the main Eurovision Song Contest earlier in 2026, performed her winning song Bangaranga and a new single, Bad Idea, before handing Carmelle the trophy. Thai star Jeff Satur also performed.

## How the voting worked

The format closely follows the European original. Half of each country's result came from a public vote and half from the 11 national juries, with additional points from a rest-of-the-world vote so fans outside the competing countries could take part.

## Can Eurovision Asia last?

Organisers spent decades talking about an Asian edition of the contest, and several earlier attempts collapsed before they reached the stage. Getting to a first final is a milestone in itself.

Whether there will be a second one is less certain. The BBC's correspondent in Bangkok reported that the arena was busy but not full and that the crowd thinned soon after the trophy was presented. Many people in the audience were existing Eurovision fans who had travelled from outside the region. It is also unclear how committed regional broadcasters are to keeping the contest going, or to expanding it beyond its first 11 countries.

For the Philippines, those questions can wait. A 19-year-old has put the country's name first in the record book of a brand-new contest, beating the host nation in front of its home crowd.
