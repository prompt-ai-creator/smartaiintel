Romelu Lukaku has retired from international football, ending a 16-year Belgium career as the country's all-time leading scorer.

The 33-year-old striker announced the decision on Instagram on Monday, with a record 94 goals in 135 games for the Red Devils. Only four men have scored more in international football: Cristiano Ronaldo (146), Lionel Messi (126), Ali Daei (108) and Sunil Chhetri (95).

"These 16 years I gave all I had for the national team. It was a nice ride and I enjoyed every single moment," Lukaku wrote. "I'm at peace with my decision to stop here. It was amazing. It was enjoyable. I'm grateful."

## No farewell game

The timing caught many by surprise. Lukaku was in the squad for this month's Nations League games but was an unused substitute on Sunday night as Belgium beat Italy 4-1 in Brussels, having been held back by a recent injury.

His last appearance for his country came in October, when he wore the captain's armband in a 4-1 defeat to France. Belgium's next game is a Nations League quarter-final in March, the first without him.

## From a 16-year-old debutant to the record books

Lukaku made his Belgium debut against Croatia on March 3, 2010, aged just 16.

He went on to play at four World Cups, in 2014, 2018, 2022 and 2026, and three European Championships. He was a key part of the "golden generation" alongside Kevin De Bruyne, Eden Hazard and Thibaut Courtois that finished third at the 2018 World Cup in Russia, Belgium's best World Cup finish.

This year he helped Belgium reach the World Cup quarter-finals, where they lost to Spain, the eventual champions. That generation never won a major trophy.

"To the Belgium fans, thank you for the support you gave our team," Lukaku said. "I am proud to have represented my country in 4 World Cups and 3 European Championships. I will never forget those moments."

## Tributes from teammates and the federation

Former teammates quickly paid tribute on social media. "Legend, my brother," wrote midfielder Axel Witsel. Real Madrid goalkeeper Courtois said: "Thank you for everything my bro. So many amazing memories."

Simon Mignolet, Belgium's technical director and Lukaku's former international teammate, called him "a true leader, with a strong personality and an essential role in the dressing room."

"We respect his decision and are deeply grateful to him for everything he has brought to our national team," Mignolet said. "We must now turn our attention to the future and prepare for the Nations League quarter-finals."

The Royal Belgian Football Association said Lukaku was "much more than just a goalscorer," adding that his personality, leadership and connection to the team had earned him "a special place in the history of the Belgian Red Devils."

## What's next for Lukaku

Lukaku is not retiring from the game. He will keep playing club football for Fenerbahce in Turkey, where he moved last summer from Napoli. He previously played for Chelsea, Manchester United, Everton and Inter Milan.

For head coach Mark van Bommel, the task now is building a Belgium attack without the man who scored more goals for the country than anyone before him.
