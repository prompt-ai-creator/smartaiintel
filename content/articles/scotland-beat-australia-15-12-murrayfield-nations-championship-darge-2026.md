Scotland ground out a 15-12 win over Australia in the Nations Championship at Murrayfield on Sunday, surviving a late Wallabies fightback in heavy rain to lift the Hopetoun Cup.

It was not pretty. Heavy rain shortly before kick-off turned the match into a kicking and defensive battle, and neither side scored a try for long spells of the second half. But a week after a disappointing defeat to New Zealand, Scotland found a way to win.

Stand-in captain Rory Darge, leading the side after centre and regular captain Sione Tuipulotu withdrew late, was named player of the match.

## Fast start in the wet

Scotland struck first. Prop Zander Fagerson barged over in the 8th minute, but Australia replied within three minutes through wing Harry Potter.

Back-row Jack Dempsey, who switched allegiance from Australia to Scotland, scored the home side's second try in the 27th minute against the country he used to represent. Finn Russell converted to make it 12-5, and that was still the score at half-time.

Both teams had first-half scores ruled out, and Australia's Pollard was shown a yellow card for a high challenge on Scotland full-back Blair Kinghorn.

## A tense finish

The second half became what BBC pundits described as a "game of chess", with both sides box-kicking and trying to win territory rather than run the ball.

With six minutes left and Scotland leading by seven, Darge chose to take the points from a penalty rather than kick to the corner. After a short discussion with Russell, the fly-half kicked it to make it 15-5.

That decision mattered. Max Jorgensen raced away on the right in the 79th minute and Ben Donaldson converted, cutting the gap to three points with the clock almost red.

Australia had one final chance from the restart. Wallabies players poured forward, but wing Darcy Graham made a scrambling, game-saving tackle to halt the attack. Moments later Blair Kinghorn carried the ball out of play to end the match, and Graham's tackle was celebrated like a try.

## What they said

Head coach Gregor Townsend praised his side's work without the ball. "Defensively, that's one of the best performances I've seen in the last couple of years," he told ITV.

Darge said: "It was some shift from the boys. We knew Australia would respond after last week, and we were disappointed after last week's performance."

Centre Tom Jordan, who came in for Tuipulotu, joked that he had been "bulking up all week" to try to match his team-mate's size.

BBC Scotland's Tom English summed it up: Scotland "won ugly today".

## Debut and injuries

Newcastle's Ollie Leatherbarrow made a winning Test debut. Lock Scott Cummings picked up a knee injury, and Townsend said Tuipulotu is unlikely to be fit for next week.

## Next up: Japan

Scotland face Japan at Murrayfield next weekend on a short six-day turnaround. Townsend said he expected to "change it up a bit", given that several replacements came on earlier than planned.
