SpaceX announced Thursday, Nov. 12, 2026, plans to invest more than $74 billion across two massive artificial intelligence supercomputing campuses—committing $24 billion at Starbase, Texas, and roughly $50 billion in Cochise County, Arizona.

## Key numbers behind SpaceX's $74 billion supercompute expansion

- **Total planned capital commitment:** Over `$74 billion` split across two U.S. Southwest supercomputing hubs supporting Elon Musk's goal of deploying at least `10 gigawatts` of AI computing capacity in 2027.
- **Cochise County, Arizona campus:** Roughly `$50 billion` in planned investment—subject to Cochise County regulatory approval—projected to generate `5,500` construction jobs and `1,000` permanent full-time roles.
- **Starbase, Texas campus:** Approximately `$24 billion` for initial phases on a `444-acre` tract near the Port of Brownsville, paired with a dedicated `35-turbine` natural-gas power plant and a proposed eight-mile `"Starpipe"` gas pipeline.
- **Key approval date in Texas:** Starbase city commissioners scheduled a public hearing for `7:00 p.m. on Dec. 9, 2026`, to vote on a sweeping property-tax abatement and economic development agreement.
- **Balance-sheet scale:** The `$24 billion` Texas phase alone equals roughly `17 times` the `$1.39 billion` value of SpaceX's `18,712` Bitcoin treasury reserve disclosed following its 2026 public listing (`SPCX`).

## How will SpaceX power the $24 billion Starbase facility without the Texas grid?

Connecting multi-gigawatt AI data centers to the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (`ERCOT`) grid now faces multi-year interconnection queues—and in August 2026, Texas Gov. Greg Abbott ordered grid operators to pause new data-center connection requests pending reliability audits amid rising consumer utility bills.

To bypass grid bottlenecks completely, SpaceX designed the Starbase supercomputing campus to generate its own behind-the-meter electricity. Municipal filings show the company plans to build, own, and operate an on-site gas-fired power plant alongside the compute halls, fed by an eight-mile natural-gas pipeline proposed by affiliate Lone Star Mineral Development with a target January 2027 connection window.

The project also navigated local zoning pushback via rapid municipal boundary changes. In August, the City of Brownsville agreed at SpaceX's urging to disannex the 444-acre parcel—shortly before Brownsville enacted a 180-day citywide data-center moratorium last week—allowing the neighboring company-aligned municipality of Starbase (population ~500) to annex the land in September and schedule the Dec. 9 tax-break vote before incoming Texas Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa takes office.

## Why is SpaceX committing $50 billion to Cochise County, Arizona?

Thursday's surprise disclosure of a second, twice-as-large $50 billion campus in southeastern Arizona's Cochise County signals that Musk is turning SpaceX's post-IPO balance sheet into one of the world's largest sovereign-scale AI infrastructure builders, competing head-on with [hyperscale hybrid AI factories](/articles/red-hat-nvidia-hybrid-ai-factory-google-tpu-8tx-8i-max-chips/) from Microsoft, Google, Amazon, and Meta.

Spreading 10 gigawatts of planned 2027 supercomputing capacity across both South Texas and rural Arizona gives SpaceX geographic redundancy outside Gulf Coast hurricane corridors, abundant desert land for high-density power and cooling, and political leverage across two states as local environmental coalitions in Cameron County press county commissioners to oppose gas-turbine emissions near Brownsville.
