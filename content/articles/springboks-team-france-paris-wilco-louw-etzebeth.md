South Africa head coach Rassie Erasmus has resisted sweeping rotation for Friday night's blockbuster Nations Championship clash against France at the Stade de France, making just two alterations to his matchday 23 while omitting 147 cap veteran lock Eben Etzebeth despite the towering second rower linking up with the squad in Paris. According to [The National's team report](https://thenational.co.za/sport/2026-11-10-rassie-erasmus-defends-minimal-changes-as-fresh-springboks-prepare-for-france-showdown/), tighthead prop Wilco Louw steps into the starting front row and experienced scrumhalf Cobus Reinach replaces Grant Williams among the replacements following last Saturday's 66 to 7 demolition of Italy in Turin.

Both South Africa and France enter Friday's floodlit encounter atop their respective hemisphere standings after round one of rugby's inaugural Nations Championship, lending genuine tournament stakes to one of world sport's most ferocious modern rivalries in a week when [Paris has already stood at the center of high level international attention](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/g7-paris-ukraine-talks-abu-dhabi-us-russia-negotiations/). Ever since South Africa edged hosts France by a single point at the exact same Saint Denis stadium in their epic 2023 Rugby World Cup quarter final, meetings between the reigning world champions and Fabien Galthie's Les Bleus have carried electric physical edge.

Some outside observers expected Erasmus to refresh half his pack given the nominal six day gap between Saturday's run out in northern Italy and Friday night's kick off in Paris. Instead, the Springbok boss insisted on Tuesday that keeping continuity was a calculated physiological decision rather than a fitness gamble.

## Why Erasmus believes his Turin starters remain fresh for Stade de France

Explaining his selection thinking in Paris, Erasmus pointed directly to cumulative club workloads and kick off scheduling to dismiss fears of heavy legs. Because South Africa kicked off at one o'clock on Saturday afternoon in Turin and flew straight to Paris while France played a bruising nine o'clock Saturday night Test, Erasmus noted that the Springboks effectively enjoy close to a full seven day recovery cycle before Friday evening's clash.

Even more decisive was the light seasonal mileage carried by South Africa's Japan based contingent, including Damian de Allende, Jesse Kriel, Pieter Steph du Toit, Lood de Jager, Malcolm Marx, and replacement playmaker Manie Libbok. Erasmus emphasized that Saturday's ten try romp past Italy had not been among the most taxing physical battles his squad has endured, allowing coaches to withdraw key backs like de Allende and fullback Damian Willemse early so few regulars logged heavy eighty minute tolls.

Leaving out Etzebeth, South Africa's most capped Test player in history with 147 appearances, underscored that refusal to rush returning stars before training benchmarks are met. While Etzebeth has rejoined camp in the French capital, Erasmus preferred locking continuity with Ruan Nortje partnering Lood de Jager in the engine room and Franco Mostert covering from the bench, especially with Salmaan Moerat still nursing a back spasm. Canan Moodie, Grant Williams, Marco van Staden, Emmanuel Tshituka, Zachary Porthen, and young utility forward Riley Norton were all singled out by Erasmus as unlucky to miss selection in a fiercely contested camp.

## Full Springboks matchday 23 and scrum battle against injury hit Les Bleus

Captain Siya Kolisi leads an imposing starting fifteen featuring Damian Willemse at fullback, Kurt Lee Arendse and Ethan Hooker on the wings, Jesse Kriel and Damian de Allende in a battle hardened midfield, and exciting young flyhalf Sacha Feinberg Mngomezulu partnering scrumhalf Morne van den Berg at halfback. Up front, loosehead Ox Nche and hooker Malcolm Marx pack down alongside promoted tighthead Wilco Louw, behind de Jager, Nortje, Kolisi, flanker Pieter Steph du Toit, and number eight Jasper Wiese.

Waiting on a powerful six two bench split of forwards and backs are front rowers Jan Hendrik Wessels, Gerhard Steenekamp, and Thomas du Toit, locks and loose forwards Franco Mostert, Cameron Hanekom, and Paul de Villiers, plus halfback veterans Cobus Reinach and Manie Libbok.

That settled South African spine confronts a French squad wrestling with an extraordinary injury crisis at flyhalf, where top playmaking options Romain Ntamack, Matthieu Jalibert, Thomas Ramos, and Antoine Hastoy have all been sidelined. Erasmus admitted on Tuesday that South Africa's analysts are unsure whom France will deploy in the number ten jersey, noting rumors that Galthie could even shift a centre into the pivot slot alongside inspirational captain and scrumhalf Antoine Dupont.

Rather than assuming France's playmaking absences will blunt the hosts, Erasmus warned his squad to brace for a lighter, faster French pack that moves the ball wide while still leaning heavily on set piece mauling. Springbok data analysts highlighted that France mauled seventy percent of their lineouts last year, including exit lineouts deep inside their own territory, guaranteeing a brutal forward collision from the opening whistle under the Stade de France lights on Friday night.

## Set piece supremacy and the tactical chess match around Sacha Feinberg Mngomezulu

Promoting tighthead powerhouse Wilco Louw straight into the number three jersey alongside loosehead Ox Nche and dynamic hooker Malcolm Marx telegraphs exactly where Erasmus intends to squeeze France first on Friday evening. Scrummaging penalties inside French territory serve a dual purpose in Paris: they quieten one of world rugby's loudest partisan crowds and give rising flyhalf star Sacha Feinberg Mngomezulu front foot ball to attack wide channels or slot three pointers from distance.

Handing Feinberg Mngomezulu the starting ten shirt in Saint Denis ahead of World Cup winning playmaker Manie Libbok, who waits as a second half impact finisher in jersey twenty three alongside veteran scrumhalf Cobus Reinach, underlines Erasmus's faith in the young pivot's temperament under extreme northern hemisphere pressure. Surrounded by ninety plus cap calm from inside centre Damian de Allende, outside centre Jesse Kriel, and fullback Damian Willemse, Feinberg Mngomezulu has ample playmaking support whenever Antoine Dupont leads French blitz rushes around the breakdown.

Unloading Thomas du Toit, Gerhard Steenekamp, Jan Hendrik Wessels, Franco Mostert, Cameron Hanekom, and numbers specialist Paul de Villiers off a six forward bench during the final half hour remains South Africa's signature closing weapon. Against a French matchday group stretched thin at flyhalf without Ntamack, Jalibert, Ramos, and Hastoy, surviving eighty minutes of relentless Springbok set piece and breakdown pressure will demand near flawless discipline from Galthie's hosts.

## Nations Championship standings on the line before Dublin trip

With the inaugural Nations Championship pitting southern and northern hemisphere heavyweights against one another for table points toward finals qualification rather than standalone autumn friendlies, Friday's clash carries tangible silverware consequences. Having opened their European tour with maximum momentum in Turin while Italy and Fiji sit winless at the bottom of their respective conference tables, victory at the Stade de France would cement South Africa as clear southern hemisphere pacesetters before the squad travels onward toward Ireland.

Keeping Etzebeth wrapped in cotton wool in Paris ensures the 147 cap enforcer can complete full contact preparation for the remaining November schedule rather than risking tightness in a fixture where Nortje, de Jager, and Mostert already provide elite lineout calling. Backed by rested legs from Japan club duty and virtually a full week's recovery since Saturday afternoon in Italy, Erasmus's settled twenty three arrive in Paris primed for eighty ferocious minutes.
