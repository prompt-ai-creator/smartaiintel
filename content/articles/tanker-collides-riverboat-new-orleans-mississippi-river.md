An asphalt tanker collided with a river passenger boat on the Mississippi River near downtown New Orleans on Friday, capsizing the vessel and triggering a large-scale rescue operation.

## What happened

The **Atlantic Asphalt** tanker struck the **American Heritage** riverboat — owned by American Cruise Lines — around **12:30 p.m. local time Friday**, Nov. 13, 2026, near the Port of New Orleans administration building. The passenger vessel had been docked for about two hours as the crew prepared for disembarkation when the collision occurred.

"The tanker lost control and collided into the cruise ship," New Orleans Mayor Helena Moreno said at a news conference Friday afternoon. "The passengers began disembarking from the ship after that collision. But while that was happening, the cruise ship began to sink, and then eventually, the cruise ship toppled over and flipped."

## Rescue operation

Nearly 200 people were aboard the American Heritage at the time of impact. **All were accounted for and rescued** — what Mayor Moreno called a **"miracle on the Mississippi."**

- **30+ people injured**, including 30 passengers and two tanker officers
- All injured were hospitalized with **non-critical injuries**
- **No fatalities** and **no children** involved, according to authorities
- A three-alarm response dispatched **23 units and 57 personnel**
- Some passengers were pulled from the water; others were transferred to rescue boats

## Investigation

The **National Transportation Safety Board** is launching an investigation team expected to arrive Saturday morning. The U.S. Coast Guard said the tanker appears to have suffered "some type of a loss of steering" before the collision, though the exact cause remains under investigation.

The **Mississippi River remains closed** between mile markers 93 and 96 while responders assess the situation. The riverboat is "significantly damaged," with only a small portion remaining above the water line, according to U.S. Coast Guard Capt. Melanie Burnham.

## Context

The collision comes on the same day as the FAA's nationwide ground stop, which was lifted Friday night after nearly five hours of halted departures. Both incidents involved transportation infrastructure failures, though officials said there is no indication the two events are related.
