Tesla will have to refund deposits to many Cybertruck customers because it cannot build enough vehicles to fulfil all orders before production ends, Elon Musk said Monday.

The company will try to produce as many trucks as possible before closing the line in about eight months, Musk wrote on X, according to [Reuters’ report on his comments](https://srnnews.com/tesla-to-refund-many-cybertruck-deposits-as-demand-exceeds-production-musk-says/). He said deposits would have to be returned to many customers who would otherwise remain in the queue.

The refund warning adds a new consequence to Tesla’s previously announced plan to wind down Cybertruck production at its Texas factory. The decision to end production was announced earlier; Monday’s remarks concern what happens to customers still waiting for a vehicle.

## Orders exceed the remaining capacity

Musk’s comments put the emphasis on how many trucks Tesla can build before the line closes, rather than on whether every reservation will become a sale.

Reuters reported that Tesla planned to add a production shift to help fulfil outstanding orders. Even with that effort, Musk’s latest statement indicates that some customers will not receive a truck before manufacturing ends.

The [initial Reuters dispatch](https://za.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/tesla-to-refund-many-cybertruck-deposits-as-demand-exceeds-production-musk-says-4560450) did not specify how many customers would receive refunds or set out a payment schedule. The published reporting also does not establish which orders Tesla will prioritize.

That leaves customers needing individual information from the company about whether their reservation can still be fulfilled. Musk’s statement is not a detailed refund procedure and should not be treated as a guarantee of a particular payment date.

## Space for the Cybercab

The production wind down is intended to free resources for Tesla’s Cybercab robotaxi, Reuters reported. Musk had previously put the end of Cybertruck production around the middle of 2027.

His reference to about eight months is an approximate timetable, not a confirmed final production day. It leaves room for the company to provide more precise information as the factory transition approaches.

The change places the Cybertruck within a wider reallocation of Tesla’s manufacturing resources. It does not by itself establish when the Cybercab will reach a particular production rate or what revenue the new vehicle will generate.

Tesla began Cybertruck deliveries in late 2023. Reuters, citing Kelley Blue Book, reported US sales of 20,237 vehicles in 2025, down 48% from the previous year and well below Musk’s earlier projection of annual sales reaching 250,000.

That sales history and the current backlog describe different things. Orders can exceed the capacity left before a planned closure even when a model has fallen short of earlier volume ambitions.

## What customers need next

The next practical step is clearer communication on which reservations can be fulfilled, which will be refunded and when customers can expect a decision.

The financial size of the refunds cannot be calculated from Musk’s comments alone. The reporting does not provide the number of affected deposits or a total amount owed.

Until Tesla supplies those details, the central message is narrower: the company expects some customers waiting for a Cybertruck to receive their money back rather than a vehicle.
