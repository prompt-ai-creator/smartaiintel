An American strategist who sat down with President Bola Tinubu at the Presidential Villa this week works on a multimillion-dollar Nigerian government lobbying contract in Washington, US Justice Department filings show, and Nigerians are asking who paid for what.

Diana London, CEO of the Florida-based firm London Global Strategies, interviewed Tinubu in Abuja on Tuesday, Nov. 17. Photos shared afterward by presidential aides and supporters presented her as an independent American journalist praising Tinubu's record and his security partnership with Washington. Within a day, filings under the US Foreign Agents Registration Act, or FARA, showed she is registered as working on Nigeria's account with the lobbying firm DCI Group.

## What the US filings show

Nigeria hired DCI Group under a **$9 million** agreement signed through Aster Legal, a law firm in Kaduna that represents National Security Adviser Nuhu Ribadu, according to a review of the filings by [TruthNigeria](https://truthnigeria.com/2026/11/u-s-filings-show-lobbyist-who-interviewed-tinubu-was-paid-under-nigerian-government-contract/). The contract sets a monthly retainer of $750,000, "with payments made in two six-month installments of $4.5 million each."

DCI Group's supplemental statements record **$662,000** paid to London Global Strategies: $75,000 as a December 2025 retainer and $375,000 as a January to May 2026 retainer, both on Dec. 23, 2025, plus $212,000 for expenses on Aug. 4, 2026. The filings also show a second retainer payment of about $4.7 million from Aster Legal in July 2026, [Daily Times Nigeria reported](https://dailytimesng.com/diana-london-denies-4-5m-tinubu-interview-payment-amid-us-fara-filings/). The filings are public in the Justice Department's FARA database under DCI Group's registration number, 6278.

In her own registration, filed Dec. 18, 2025, London described the work this way: "Consultant shall assist the Nigerian government through Aster Legal in communicating its actions to protect Nigerian Christian communities and maintaining U.S support in countering West African jihadist groups and other destabilizing elements."

## London denies the $4.5 million claim

The controversy started when the Abuja-based Peoples Gazette reported that London had been paid $4.5 million by Ribadu, a figure that spread quickly on Nigerian social media. The filings show that sum was DCI Group's first contract installment, not a payment to London personally.

London rejected the claim on X. "I unequivocally deny the claim that Nigeria's government nor anyone else in Nigeria paid me $4.5 million. I went to Nigeria to interview President Tinubu for parts of a documentary and see Nigeria for myself. This SMEAR CAMPAIGN can make noise, but can't change the facts or stop me from speaking the truth," she wrote, [Punch reported](https://punchng.com/us-strategist-denies-4-5m-payment-for-tinubu-interview/).

Before that, she deleted posts praising the Nigeria–US security partnership and restricted who could reply to her on X, [Punch found](https://punchng.com/american-strategist-restricts-x-comment-after-backlash-over-tinubu-interview/). Her statement did not say whether the Tinubu interview was connected to the lobbying work her firm was paid for, [TheNigeriaLawyer noted](https://thenigerialawyer.com/i-went-to-nigeria-to-interview-president-tinubu-us-strategist-diana-london-denies-receiving-4-5m-amid-lobbying-contract-controversy/). The filings themselves do not show that the interview was paid for.

## Why the contract exists

The lobbying push followed Washington's redesignation of Nigeria as a Country of Particular Concern over religious freedom, over allegations of mass killings of Christians, a designation announced by President Donald Trump. Nigeria and the US have since built a military partnership against armed groups in the north.

At a Nov. 12 hearing of the House Foreign Affairs Africa subcommittee, chaired by Rep. Chris Smith, State Department official Frank Garcia said the killings had subsided and Nigeria was taking religious violence more seriously, [The Tablet reported](https://www.thetablet.co.uk/news/nigeria-has-acted-against-killing-of-christians-say-us-officials/). Another official, Riley Barnes, was more cautious. "Abuja carries the burden of proof, and there is still more work to be done," he said.

TruthNigeria also reported a separate $120,000-a-month Nigerian contract with Valcour Consulting. Registering under FARA is a disclosure requirement, not a finding of wrongdoing, and nothing in the filings suggests the payments broke US law.

## Nigerians want answers

At home, the debate is about public money and transparency, as many Nigerians face high living costs and insecurity. Former Vice President Atiku Abubakar called on the Presidency to publish the full lobbying contract, disclose all payments and invoices, and release the complete, unedited recording of Tinubu's interview, [Daily Times Nigeria reported](https://dailytimesng.com/diana-london-denies-4-5m-tinubu-interview-payment-amid-us-fara-filings/). The opposition ADC party also asked Tinubu to explain the payment.

Critics have also questioned why the Presidency gave a rare sit-down interview to a foreign lobbyist rather than Nigerian journalists. As of the latest reports, the Presidency had not publicly explained whether the interview was arranged through the DCI Group contract. Nigeria's spending in Washington comes as US support to Africa shifts, including the [closure of USAID](/articles/usaid-closure-global-health-impact-2026/) and its global health programs, and as Nigerians abroad face tougher checks, with Britain now [refusing 84% of Nigerian students it calls to a visa interview](/articles/uk-student-visa-refusals-nigeria-84-percent-credibility-interview-ukvi-2026/).
