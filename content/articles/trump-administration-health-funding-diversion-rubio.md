The Trump administration plans to redirect or withhold nearly all of the **$605 million** Congress allocated for international family planning aid this year, according to an internal US State Department budget report and a memo to lawmakers obtained by CNN.

## The diversion

The budget plans also involve moving nearly a billion dollars into a **global health innovation fund benefiting US corporations**, while sharply cutting global health funding for low- and middle-income countries.

Tens of millions of the family planning funding are being diverted to support the **"energy security needs"** of US partners facing market disruptions amid the wars in Iran and Ukraine, according to a September memo signed by Secretary of State **Marco Rubio**.

## Countries hit hardest

Eight countries have had their health funding cut to zero since 2024:

- Afghanistan
- Central African Republic
- Gambia
- South Africa
- Somalia
- Sudan
- Timor-Leste
- Yemen

South Africa, Tanzania, and Zimbabwe each face **more than $100 million in cuts**.

## Congressional backlash

The actions have drawn sharp criticism from nonprofit organizations and lawmakers who argue the administration is overstepping its authority, given that Congress holds the power of the purse.

**"The Trump administration's refusal to spend Congressionally-appropriated funds for international family planning, as required by law, is shameful and dangerous,"** said Congresswoman **Lois Frankel** (D-FL), who serves on the House Appropriations Committee.

**"To see dollars that have been fought for and written into the law by Congress moved away or siphoned away to energy security needs, or to US private companies with a profit margin, or out of really important public health programs … I think it is devastating to see that happen,"** said **Vincent Lin**, associate director of health policy and advocacy at Partners In Health.

## Broader context

In fiscal year 2024, US investment in global family planning is estimated to have **prevented 34,000 maternal deaths**, according to the Guttmacher Institute.

The administration has previously redirected **$1.35 billion** in global health funding to cover the cost of closing the US Agency for International Development (USAID).

Public Citizen has **sued the State Department** in an effort to force the release of records surrounding bilateral global health agreements. The lawsuit is ongoing in federal court.
