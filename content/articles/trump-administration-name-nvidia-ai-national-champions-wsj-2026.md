The Trump administration plans to name Nvidia and more than two dozen other American artificial intelligence companies as "national champions", giving them government backing to win business overseas against Chinese rivals, The Wall Street Journal reported on Sunday.

Amazon, Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic are also on the list, according to the report, which cited people familiar with the plans. The full list has not been made public.

The move would put the government's weight behind Nvidia at the most turbulent moment in its history, days after [Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/jensen-huang-resigns-nvidia-ceo-colette-kress-harvey-jones-601b-wipeout/) and as Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent [demands a "dramatic shakeup"](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/bessent-nvidia-dramatic-shakeup-outside-ceo-board-10-days-2026/) at the company.

## What the label would bring

According to accounts of the Journal's report, companies designated as national champions would get:

- **Faster export licences** for chips and other technology
- **Government support** for their operations abroad
- **Access to federal financing**

The plan would also bundle US chips, AI models and other technology into packages that foreign governments and businesses could buy. The aim is to offer countries a ready-made American alternative to Chinese suppliers such as Huawei.

Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick is expected to unveil the designations, possibly within a week, the Journal reported, though the timing could still change.

Reuters, which relayed the story, said it could not immediately verify it. No comment from the White House, the Commerce Department or the companies had been reported.

## Built on a 2025 order

The idea builds on an executive order President Trump signed on July 23, 2025, the day of the White House AI summit in Washington. That order, known as Executive Order 14320, set up the American AI Exports Program to promote sales of "full-stack" US AI technology abroad, from chips to software.

The national champions plan would go further by naming specific companies the government wants to promote.

It also points in the opposite direction from some lawmakers in Congress, who have been moving toward tighter rules on AI chip exports, according to the Financial Times. For the companies on the list, the reported plan would speed up export licensing rather than slow it down.

## Why it matters for Nvidia

Nvidia is the most obvious name on the list. Its chips are the part of the American AI stack that foreign buyers want most.

But the timing is awkward. Nvidia is run by interim CEO Colette Kress while its board investigates the conduct that led to Huang's resignation. Bessent has given the board 10 days to answer questions about whether Nvidia chips reached China through shell companies.

Inside the administration, Lutnick himself has faced scrutiny over Nvidia. Axios reported last week that he [stalled a crackdown on Nvidia chip sales to China three times](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/howard-lutnick-stalls-trump-nvidia-china-chip-crackdown-bessent-musk/) before a clash with Bessent. Now Lutnick is the official expected to announce Nvidia's new status.

Outside the company, a campaign to [bring Huang back as CEO](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/jensen-huang-weighs-comeback-nvidia-ceo-allies-war-room-ft-2026/) has drawn more than 165,000 signatures, including Elon Musk's.

## Anthropic on the list

The report also names Anthropic, the maker of the Claude AI models, which is preparing for one of the biggest stock market listings of the year. Its [IPO was reported to be heavily oversubscribed](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/anthropic-ipo-six-times-oversubscribed-90-billion-raise-nvidia-anchor/) last week.

Anthropic's inclusion alongside OpenAI suggests the administration wants to promote American AI model makers abroad as well as chipmakers and cloud companies.

## Markets on edge before Nvidia earnings

The report landed as investors braced for a big week. US stock futures slipped early on Monday, and Reuters reported that a selloff in AI stocks had resumed ahead of Nvidia's results.

Nvidia reports quarterly earnings on Tuesday, November 17, its first results since Huang's departure. Kress will lead the call. Analysts are expected to ask about the leadership search, China and whether the national champions plan changes the outlook for overseas sales.
