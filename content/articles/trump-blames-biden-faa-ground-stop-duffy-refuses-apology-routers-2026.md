President Donald Trump has blamed Joe Biden for Friday's nationwide FAA ground stop, breaking almost two days of silence on the outage that halted flights at every US airport and stranded hundreds of thousands of travelers.

"The Biden Administration was an FAA disaster. Everything they touched turned bad," Trump wrote on Truth Social on Sunday. "Big money spent unwisely, making an old and broken system worse than when they began."

He said his administration has "the knowledge and plan for the Flight Control Systems," adding: "we need Congress to give the money, FAST! Our FAA is doing a great job, and is ready to go."

## A second post: "RIDICULOUS"

About 20 minutes later, Trump posted again.

"If Congress approves the desperately needed money for Air Traffic Control, we can get it built quickly and efficiently. It will be the BEST SYSTEM anywhere in the World," he wrote. "Much of our current air control system is 50 years old - RIDICULOUS!" He said the work "should be done all at once, not in drips and drabs" and repeated that the Biden administration "set it back years."

Before Sunday, Trump's only reported comment had been a brief remark to a Fox News reporter late Friday blaming "old broken stuff". Critics noted that on Saturday he posted about other topics, including the [judge who blocked his DC golf course plan](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/judge-blocks-trump-closure-east-potomac-golf-course-trash-island-dc-2026/).

## Duffy: "I can't apologize"

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy toured the Sunday news shows to defend the administration.

Asked on NBC's *Meet the Press* whether he owed the public an apology, he said: "I can't apologize for what was passed off to us." He added: "It happened on my watch, but we have to be clear: I am moving at an incredible speed that no one has done at the FAA in decades."

On CNN's *State of the Union*, Dana Bash asked why Americans had not heard from the president sooner. Duffy said he had been "in constant contact" with Trump, who "was fully engaged because this is important to him and it's important to America."

## Routers in Arkansas and Texas

Investigators are now focused on two routers. Duffy said a router at an FAA site in El Dorado, Arkansas, set off Friday's outage, and that a second router in Texas also flooded the network with data.

Officials believe a feature that broadcast data more widely than necessary overwhelmed parts of the system. Duffy said that broadcast capability has been switched off across air traffic control routers while the investigation continues.

The routers were being installed as part of work led by contractor L3Harris to [replace old copper lines with fiber](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/copper-wire-upgrade-caused-us-ground-stop-duffy-l3harris-2026/). On Saturday Duffy said either faulty equipment or contractor error was to blame. The router maker told The Wall Street Journal that its devices "did not fail" and worked as intended.

Officials have said they do not believe a cyberattack was involved.

## The money fight

Duffy asked Congress for $30 billion in a letter on Thursday, the day before the outage. On Friday he raised that to $50 billion. Asked about the jump, he said he was trying to "get a number that I think the Congress will give me," and promised an itemized request next week.

Democrats say the administration has not used the money it already has. Congress approved $12.5 billion for air traffic control upgrades in 2025. Representatives Rick Larsen and André Carson said only about $1 billion has been spent. "Explain to Congress, again, why they need more money?" they said in a joint statement.

Senator Tammy Duckworth of Illinois wrote: "Apparently, there's always been money for vanity projects. But none to fix our air traffic control system?" Senator Chris Van Hollen said it was "really rich" for Duffy to point fingers at Congress.

Duffy said on Fox News that the approved money "is all accounted for" and will be spent by the end of 2028, but that "I have no money for a new network."

## How bad it was

The FAA ordered the [nationwide ground stop](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/faa-orders-nationwide-ground-stop-all-us-airports-atc-outage/) on Friday after a communications failure hit air traffic control centers. It was only the second nationwide stop since the September 11 attacks and was [lifted after several hours](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/faa-lifts-nationwide-ground-stop-flights-resume-airspace-reopened/).

More than 14,000 flights were canceled on Friday and Saturday, according to ABC News, with hundreds more on Sunday. FAA officials said they expected air travel to be largely back to normal by the end of Sunday.
