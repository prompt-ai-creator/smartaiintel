A Texas judge nominated by President Donald Trump to a lifetime federal judgeship struggled to describe the 22nd Amendment at his Senate confirmation hearing on Wednesday, then declined to say whether the president who nominated him can run for a third term.

The exchange between James Sullivan and Sen. Chris Coons, a Delaware Democrat, spread widely online after the Senate Judiciary Committee hearing.

## 'Oh boy, oh boy'

"Mr. Sullivan, can you just tell me what the 22nd Amendment provides?" Coons asked.

"Senator, if I'm remembering correctly, and I carry it over my heart because sometimes it isn't in my brain," Sullivan began, referring to a pocket copy of the Constitution. "But if I'm remembering it correctly, it is, uh... Oh boy, oh boy!"

Coons helped him: "No person shall be elected..."

When Coons asked whether Trump had served two terms, Sullivan said: "President Trump has been certified as the winner in the 2016 and the 2024..."

## 'A very disappointing answer'

Coons then asked directly: "Is he eligible to run for president again in 2028?"

"So, Senator, as a nominee for a federal district court, it would be a violation, I believe, of Canon 5 for me to opine on something like that," Sullivan said, referring to the judicial code of conduct.

"That's a very disappointing answer," Coons replied. "It strikes me as just bracingly clear on the face of it. A textualist, anyone who has read the Constitution and is sitting before me for confirmation for a lifetime appointment, I don't think it is the subject of political debate."

Coons added that the question "is only unresolved in the mind of President Trump, who has repeatedly said that he's thinking he might run for a third time. But the amendment is bracingly clear: 'No person shall be elected to the office of the president more than twice.'"

Sullivan said he would look "to the text of the provision and the original public meaning of that text," and thanked Coons for "the refresher."

## Another nominee answered in one sentence

Coons put the same question to William Lambert, Trump's nominee for the US District Court for the District of South Carolina. Lambert did not hesitate.

"Senator, the text of the 22nd Amendment seems clear that no one may serve a third term," Lambert said.

## Who is James Sullivan?

Sullivan, 45, is a Texas Supreme Court justice who lost his reelection bid earlier this month. Trump then nominated him to a lifetime seat on the US District Court for the Western District of Texas, People reported.

His hearing comes as Senate Republicans race to confirm Trump's nominees before Democrats take control of the chamber in January, after [Democrats won a 56-seat majority](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/mary-peltola-wins-alaska-senate-beats-dan-sullivan-democrats-56-seats-2026/) in the midterms. Not every nominee is getting through: on Tuesday, the White House withdrew its pick to lead the Food and Drug Administration, Heidi Overton, amid doubts about her support in the Senate.

## What the 22nd Amendment says

The 22nd Amendment was ratified in 1951, after Franklin D. Roosevelt won four presidential elections. It says, in part: "No person shall be elected to the office of the President more than twice."

Trump, 80, won the 2016 and 2024 elections and has repeatedly suggested he might run again. Changing the two-term limit would require a new constitutional amendment, approved by two-thirds of both chambers of Congress and ratified by three-quarters of the states.
