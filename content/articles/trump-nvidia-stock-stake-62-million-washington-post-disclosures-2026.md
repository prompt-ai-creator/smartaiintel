President Donald Trump's stake in Nvidia grew to as much as $62 million this year, according to a Washington Post review of his financial disclosures published on Monday.

The trades Trump has reported this year suggest he held between $13 million and $62 million in Nvidia stock as of the end of August, the Post found. That compares with Nvidia holdings worth $615,000 to $1.3 million that he reported at the end of 2024.

The true figure today could be different. The disclosures do not yet cover trades from the past two months because of reporting lags, the Post said. Nvidia's share price also climbed between the end of 2025 and August.

## Why the ranges are so wide

Federal officials must report stock trades and holdings to the Office of Government Ethics, but the forms list values as ranges rather than exact amounts. That is why the estimate stretches from $13 million to $62 million.

Trump's disclosures have shown heavy trading in big tech stocks. Filings released in May covered more than 3,700 transactions in the first three months of 2026, worth between $220 million and $750 million in total, CNBC reported. They included a purchase of $1 million to $5 million of Nvidia stock on February 10.

The White House has said the president does not select or trade individual stocks himself and that his portfolio is handled by independent financial managers.

## A company shaped by his decisions

The Post's report focuses on how closely Nvidia's fortunes have been tied to the administration's choices.

- **China chip sales:** in December 2025, Trump said the US would let Nvidia export its H200 processors, its second-most-powerful AI chips, to approved customers in China, with the government collecting a 25% fee on the sales. The Post said the value of Trump's holdings rose sharply as the administration allowed those sales.
- **Access:** former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang had extraordinary access to the president, the Post reported, including flying with him on Air Force One on his last trip to China.
- **Enforcement:** the Post also reported that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick held off on restricting a company suspected of diverting Nvidia chips to China.
- **National champions:** on Sunday, The Wall Street Journal reported that the administration plans to name Nvidia and more than two dozen other AI companies as "national champions", a label meant to help them sell abroad.

Critics say a large personal stake in a company the president's administration regulates raises conflict-of-interest questions.

## Timing: a pivotal week for Nvidia

The report lands at a sensitive moment for the world's most valuable chipmaker. Huang resigned as CEO last week, and allies are now campaigning for his return. Chief financial officer Colette Kress is running the company on an interim basis.

Nvidia is due to report quarterly earnings on Tuesday, November 17, its first results since Huang's exit. Investors will be watching for any comment on China sales, demand for its AI chips and the leadership search.
