President Donald Trump warned Iran on Tuesday that it would face consequences "the likes of which no country has ever paid before" if it threatened Washington. His comments came after a report that US intelligence had picked up Iranian plans to target senior administration officials with a radioactive dirty bomb or a chemical weapon.

Speaking to reporters in Manila, where he is attending meetings with Asian leaders, Trump said his administration was looking into the intelligence.

"Well, if there is, they will pay a price the likes of which no country has ever paid before," Trump said when asked whether an attack was imminent. "I hope it's not, but we take it very seriously, because they happen to be crazy. This is why they can't have a nuclear weapon, by the way."

## What the intelligence report says

CNN reported on Monday, citing seven US officials familiar with the intelligence, that Iran was developing possible options to target senior Trump administration officials in Washington. The reported plans involved a radiological dispersal device, known as a dirty bomb, or a chemical weapon.

People familiar with the intelligence described it as Iran building options rather than a sign of an imminent attack. Officials were also divided over how credible the information was and whether Iran had people in place to carry out such a plot, according to the report.

The intelligence has not shown that Iran has built a dirty bomb, placed one in Washington or decided to attack.

## White House: 'No credible threats to the homeland'

The White House has sought to play down the risk of an immediate attack.

"We remain vigilant, monitoring any and all potential threats, but at this time there are no credible threats to the homeland," White House spokesperson Lauren Bis said.

The Pentagon referred questions to the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, which declined to comment.

## Iran denies the allegations

Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei called the reports "baseless allegations." He accused Washington of using unproven claims to justify further action against Tehran.

Iran's mission to the United Nations said Tehran considers the use of chemical weapons under any circumstances "wholly illegitimate" and "categorically rejects and unequivocally refutes the false and fabricated allegations."

## Cabinet members living on military bases

The report comes as several senior officials have moved into military housing in the Washington area over security concerns linked to Iran. They include Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, [Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/treasury-secretary-scott-bessent-moves-military-base-iran-threats/) and, according to reports, [Attorney General Todd Blanche](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/attorney-general-todd-blanche-military-base-bessent-rubio-iran-threats/).

Asked outside the White House on Monday whether he was worried about Iranian drones, Trump told reporters: "I'm worried about you, because if they get me, you're gone too. Be careful."

## What is a dirty bomb?

A dirty bomb is very different from a nuclear weapon. It uses ordinary explosives to scatter radioactive material instead of creating a nuclear explosion. It can injure people near the blast and contaminate the surrounding area, which can be costly and slow to clean up.

## A war that has stalled

The warning comes as the conflict between the United States and Iran remains unresolved. A US-Israeli military campaign against Iran began in February, and Washington has kept up pressure with military operations and a naval blockade. Trump has sent mixed signals, at times threatening more strikes while also saying [the war is "militarily" effectively over](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/trump-iran-war-militarily-over-qatar-talks-brent-oil-drop/).

Iran has also carried out new drone attacks on US military sites in Bahrain, including the [Riffa air base](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/iran-drones-us-radar-riffa-air-base-bahrain-sirens-hormuz-2026/).
