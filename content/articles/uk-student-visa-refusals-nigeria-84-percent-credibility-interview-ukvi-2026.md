Britain is turning away most Nigerian students it calls in for a visa interview. UK Visas and Immigration judged **84%** of Nigerian students it interviewed between May and October 2026 to be "non-credible," according to a UKVI briefing paper for universities obtained by [The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/ukvi-rejects-80-of-students-called-to-interview-as-visa-refusals-surge/), a trade publication for international education.

The same document shows Nigerians are being singled out for interviews far more often. The share of Nigerian student applicants invited to a credibility interview rose from **7% to 21%** between 2025 and 2026.

## Nearly 4 in 5 interviewed students were refused

Across all countries, UKVI held 11,415 credibility interviews in the six months to October and found 9,043 applicants non-credible, a rejection rate of about 79%. Nepalese students fared worst, with 92% rejected after interview, followed by India at 89% and Nigeria at 84%. Bangladesh and Sri Lanka each came in at 79%, and Pakistan at 73%.

UKVI said the figures covered only six months and were not necessarily representative of a full year. Most student visa applicants are never called for an interview, so the numbers show what happens to those flagged for extra checks, not the overall refusal rate for Nigerians.

## Why Nigerians are being flagged

UKVI explained the jump in interviews directly. "We have seen an increase in applications from Nigeria where further scrutiny is needed, resulting in a greater proportion of applicants being invited to attend a credibility interview," it said in the briefing, according to The PIE News. It described the change as reflecting "evolving patterns and our understanding of risk within the route."

The briefing also listed Nigeria among the countries whose students are most likely to claim asylum after arriving, behind Pakistan, India and Bangladesh. That is a key concern for the government, which has already put a brake on study visas for nationals of Afghanistan, Cameroon, Sudan and Myanmar over asylum claims.

## What gets students refused

The most common reasons for a refusal on credibility grounds, according to UKVI, were a lack of research about the chosen university, not being able to clearly explain why the student picked the UK, and failing to show how the course and institution "align with their future plans." Officers also cited weak knowledge of the course, English language ability and doubts about whether the student's funds were genuine.

Some refusals have drawn ridicule. The PIE News collected cases where students were reportedly rejected for not knowing how many trees were on their university's campus or where the nearest bus stop was. Universities have complained that the pass threshold changed without warning, and Nick Hillman, director of the Higher Education Policy Institute, told [The i Paper](https://inews.co.uk/news/education/student-visas-rejected-trees-campus-4861318) it was "abundantly clear" that the officials doing the interviews are "not fit for the job," calling it "the next migration scandal waiting to happen."

## The Home Office response

Oliver Rae, who oversees work, study and family visa services at the Home Office, told a university conference this week that the tighter checks are not a way to cap international student numbers. He acknowledged that "we don't always get the decisions right," but said the number of interviews had fallen year on year as caseworkers got better at targeting risk. "It was 6,000 students that we interviewed during the summer period," he said, according to The PIE News.

The squeeze is not limited to Britain. Irish universities are facing an estimated €8 million fee shortfall after a wave of student visa refusals, [The PIE News reported](https://thepienews.com/irish-universities-face-e8m-fee-shortfall-after-student-visa-refusals/) this week, and US employers have also pulled back on visa sponsorship, with [IBM ending US visa and green card sponsorship](/articles/ibm-ends-us-visa-green-card-sponsorship-consulting-roles/) for some consulting roles.

## What this means for Nigerian applicants

For Nigerian families paying for a UK degree, the data means an interview is now roughly a one-in-five chance, and the interview is where most refusals happen. Based on UKVI's own list of refusal reasons, applicants who are called should be ready to explain in their own words why they chose the UK over other countries, why that specific university and course, how the degree fits their career plans back home or elsewhere, and exactly where their funding comes from. Students should check the official requirements on the [GOV.UK student visa page](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa) and be wary of agents who promise guaranteed approval.

A refusal means losing the visa application fee and months of planning, so preparing for the interview matters as much as the paperwork.
