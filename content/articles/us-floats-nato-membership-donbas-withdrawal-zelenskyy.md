The United States has floated offering Ukraine NATO membership in exchange for withdrawing from the part of the Donbas region still under its control, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Friday, as Washington explores terms for ending Russia's full-scale invasion.

## The proposal

US negotiators Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff asked Ukraine whether Kyiv would concede the part of Donbas still under its control to Moscow in exchange for NATO membership, Zelenskyy told reporters. It is the first time Washington has raised such a proposal, the Financial Times reported.

"If the American side confirms precisely this to us, together with other NATO partners, then I will ask our Ukrainian society what it thinks: do we accept it or not?" Zelenskyy said. He added that he had no right to make such a decision unilaterally and that Kyiv would not recognise the occupied territories within Ukraine's internationally recognised borders, including the Donbas, as Russian.

## What is at stake

Ukraine still controls about 20% of the Donbas region. Russian forces have sustained tens of thousands of casualties this year in their attempts to seize the "fortress belt" of cities remaining under Kyiv's control.

The proposal emerged during the first trilateral talks between Russia, Ukraine and the US since discussions paused in February after the war with Iran erupted. European officials joined discussions only with the Americans and Ukrainians; Russia has refused to accept them at the negotiating table.

EU leaders agreed on Friday that a coalition of officials from France, Germany, Italy, Poland and the UK—plus the EU and NATO—would represent Europe at technical-level talks.

## Broader security package

Zelenskyy said Kyiv was discussing a broader security package with its partners, including:

- Guarantees from NATO and the EU
- Long-term military support for a strong Ukrainian army
- An internationally backed reconstruction plan

Western countries have also discussed an arrangement short of full NATO membership but with multilateral agreements mimicking the alliance's Article 5 mutual defence clause.

Denmark has pledged a €645mn support package that includes 20mm ammunition for F-16s—needed to shoot down jet-powered drones—as well as €200mn for drone production.

## Starshield satellite dispute

Zelenskyy also criticised speed limits imposed on Starshield satellite systems used by the Ukrainian military for middle-range strikes against Russian targets in occupied territory. He said the restrictions, in place for several days, have limited speeds to 161kph (100mph) and were hampering efforts to intercept Russian jet-powered drones.

Kyiv believes the restrictions were imposed to put pressure on Kyiv during the latest peace talks with Russia and strengthen Moscow's position. SpaceX denied the claim in an email, calling it "false. There has been no change."
