Confidence among America's homebuilders has fallen to its lowest level in 14 years. Mortgage rates above 7%, rising costs and a shortage of buyers are pushing nearly half of builders to cut prices, according to an industry survey released on Tuesday.

The National Association of Home Builders (NAHB)/Wells Fargo Housing Market Index fell 4 points to 25 in November. That is the lowest reading since April 2012, when the housing market was just starting to recover from the crash and foreclosure crisis. In November last year, the index stood at 38. Any reading below 50 means more builders see conditions as poor than good.

## Nearly half of builders are cutting prices

To bring buyers in, 46% of builders reduced prices in November. That is the highest share since the Covid pandemic, NAHB said.

The share of builders offering sales incentives also hit a post-Covid high of 68%. It was the 20th month in a row that at least 60% of builders used incentives such as mortgage rate buydowns.

## Buyer traffic hits a 15-year low

All three parts of the index fell in November:

- **Current sales conditions** dropped 6 points to 28.
- **Sales expectations for the next six months** fell 2 points to 30.
- **Traffic of prospective buyers** fell 2 points to 16. That is its lowest level in 15 years, if the start of the pandemic in 2020 is excluded.

Every region weakened. Based on three-month moving averages, the Northeast fell 4 points to 34, the Midwest dropped 5 points to 35, the South fell 3 points to 27 and the West declined 3 points to 23.

## Mortgage rates climb back above 7.5%

Mortgage rates eased a little in October but jumped again last week. The average 30-year fixed rate hit 7.51% on Monday, according to Mortgage News Daily, compared with 6.38% a year ago.

The weak builder reading follows last week's report that [sales of previously owned US homes fell to their lowest level since 2010](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/us-existing-home-sales-plunge-lowest-level-since-2010-mortgage-rates/), and [hotter-than-expected October inflation](https://smartaiintel.com/articles/october-us-cpi-inflation-beats-forecasts-fed-rate-hike-bets/) that has raised bets on higher interest rates.

## What builders say

"Builders are confronting multiple challenges in today's economy," said NAHB Chairman Bill Owens, a home builder and remodeler from Worthington, Ohio. "Stepped-up immigration enforcement is keeping legal workers off job sites, deepening labor shortages and driving up costs. Higher fuel prices are prompting subcontractors to raise their rates sharply, and with fewer buyers in the market, more builders are scaling back construction."

NAHB Chief Economist Robert Dietz pointed to tariffs and regulation as well as borrowing costs.

"The housing market faces a range of supply-side challenges, including building material costs pushed higher by tariffs, an ongoing shortage of construction workers and excessive regulations that undermine housing affordability," Dietz said. "Mortgage rates above 7%, softer consumer confidence and an uncertain economic outlook are holding buyers back."

## What it means for buyers

For buyers who can afford today's rates, the survey points to more room to bargain. Builders are cutting prices and adding incentives to sell homes. But fewer new homes may be built in the months ahead as builders scale back, which could keep supply tight once demand recovers.
