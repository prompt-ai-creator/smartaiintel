Warren Buffett has given away another giant slice of his fortune, donating Berkshire Hathaway shares worth about $5.5 billion to four family foundations in his annual Thanksgiving gift.

The donation, reported by CNBC on Monday, is roughly four times the size of last year's Thanksgiving gift of about $1.3 billion. It comes with the 96-year-old investor's yearly holiday letter, in which he returned to the theme of kindness.

"Last Thanksgiving I wrote that kindness is costless but also priceless," Buffett wrote. "A year later, I would only add that it compounds, and at a better rate than anything Charlie and I ever bought."

## Who gets the shares

To make the gifts, Buffett converted 6,150 of his Class A shares into 9,225,000 Class B shares. They are being split like this:

- **The Susan Thompson Buffett Foundation**, named after his late first wife: 3,690,000 Class B shares
- **The Sherwood Foundation**, run by his daughter Susie: 1,845,000 shares
- **The Howard G. Buffett Foundation**, run by his son Howard: 1,845,000 shares
- **The NoVo Foundation**, run by his son Peter: 1,845,000 shares

After the gifts, Buffett still owns 182,140 Class A shares, a stake worth roughly $162 billion, plus 1,162 Class B shares, according to Berkshire.

## 'All my Berkshire shares will be donated'

Buffett said the gift follows the plan he has laid out before: everything he owns in Berkshire will eventually go to the same four foundations.

"All my Berkshire shares will be donated to the four foundations, with the goal of growing the grants each year," he said. "Reaching this goal will largely depend on stock market conditions between now and the target date."

He also made clear the gift is a vote of confidence in his family.

"My children have given thoughtfully to worthy causes for decades, and I trust 100% both their abilities and their fidelity," he wrote.

## A nod to Charlie Munger and his teachers

The letter looked back at the people who shaped Buffett and his late business partner, Charlie Munger, who died in 2023.

"Both of us received beyond measurement costless benefits from a few teachers such as Ben Graham, Joe Rosenfield and Tom Murphy," Buffett wrote. "Because of this we not only prospered mightily but were able to pass along many of the benefits we received."

He ended with advice for readers: "This Thanksgiving, thank one of those selfless people in your own life. Better yet, be a benefactor for someone else."

## Confidence in Berkshire under Greg Abel

Buffett handed the chief executive job to Greg Abel at the start of 2026 and is now Berkshire's chairman emeritus. His son Howard succeeded him as chairman in September.

"You heard plenty from me in September about my confidence in Berkshire. That confidence is as strong as ever," Buffett wrote.

Abel praised the letter. "Warren continues to share his wisdom with us all during this holiday season. His example reflects the kindness he encourages in all of us," he said.

## A Thanksgiving tradition

Buffett began the Thanksgiving letters last year as he prepared to step down as CEO. In that first letter, in November 2025, he gave about $1.3 billion in shares to the same four foundations and said he was speeding up his gifts so his children could hand out what will essentially be his entire estate.

This year's gift is much larger, keeping Buffett among the most generous donors in the world.
